Introduction
This page describes the luminosity recommendations for physics analyses from the LuminosityGroup. This includes the luminosity tag
which is used to compute the integrated luminosity for a given dataset, and the associated systematic uncertainty to be propagated to the
analysis results. The integrated luminosity is calculated separately by each analysis team, for its specific trigger and GRL setup.
Instructions are available in the red box below.
Luminosity for physics analysis
Every analysis must calculate the luminosity for its specific setup, based on the luminosity tag, GRL, and trigger, using the ATLAS
luminosity calculator .
Please make sure to follow these steps:
use the appropriate GRL, which you can find on the GoodRunListsForAnalysis page.
use the recommended lumi tag, which you can find below.
as “physics trigger” use the trigger used in your particular analysis.

Luminosity requests
If your specific dataset is missing on this page, please contact the luminosity conveners atlas-luminosity-conveners. Luminosity requests
are further handled through the luminosity JIRA page . The JIRA ticket is created to make sure the information listed below is provided to
allow the creation of a dedicated luminosity tag:
A GRL with the list of runs and luminosity blocks which are good for analysis
The luminosity algorithm that should be used for the luminosity calculation
The JIRA ticket should indicate explicitly the name of the data set for which the luminosity should be calculated and the requested timeline.

The preliminary calibration of 2022 13.6TeV pp LHCf data uses a calibration based on the vdM scans recorded 10-11 November 2022 (run
439428), as well as stability tests based on tracking and LUCID. The default luminometer from the LUCID side is LUCID Bi2 HITOR.
Figures and tables documenting the vdM calibration are included in ATL-DAPR-PUB-2023-001 .
The following sentence can be written in public CONF notes/papers: "The uncertainty in the integrated luminosity for the LHCf data
recorded in 2022 is 1.5%. The error is composed of 1.45% from the vdM calibration~ \cite{ATL-DAPR-PUB-2023-001}, following the
methodology discussed in Ref.~ \cite{DAPR-2021-01}, using the LUCID-2 detector~\cite{LUCID2} for the primary luminosity
measurements. An uncertainty of 0.5% is added in quadrature due to the extrapolation of the LUCID measurement from the vdM calibration
run to the LHCf run, checked by comparing LUCD to tracking."

Last update for this entry 22 Feb 2017
The following sentence(s) can be written in public conf notes/paper: "The absolute luminosity scale is derived from beam-separation scans
performed in November 2012. The uncertainty in the integrated luminosity is 1.9%~\cite{DAPR-2013-01}."
The luminosity uncertainties at 8 TeV (2012) and at 7 TeV (2010+2011) should be treated as uncorrelated, for the following reasons.
- The vdM-calibration uncertainties in 2011 & 2012 are dominated by beam conditions at the time of the scan. These vary from scan
session to scan session, even at the same c.m. energy. More information on this topic can be found in W. Kozanecki's presentation to the
2012 Lumi Days workshop: https://indico.cern.ch/event/162948/session/5/contribution/18/material/0/
- The vdM-calibration uncertainties in 2010 are dominated by the bunch-current product. These are more than 5 times larger in 2010 than in
2011 & 2012, and their origin is such that the 2011/12 bunch-population uncertainty is uncorrelated with the corresponding 2010
uncertainty.
- In addition, the total uncertainty in the 2012 integrated luminosity receives large contributions from instrumental effects (long-term stability,
calibration transfer from vdM to physics conditions) that did not affect the 2010 & 2011 luminosity measurements. A tabulated comparison of
the 2011 and 2012 luminosity uncertainties can be found here:
https://indico.cern.ch/event/577659/contributions/2339494/attachments/1355174/2047736/Run1_L_2011n2012_systs_WK_14Oct16.pdf
The correlations between the ATLAS & CMS luminosity uncertainties at 7 and 8 TeV were finalized, and agreed upon between ATLAS &
CMS, during the LLCMWG meeting of 6 Feb 2017. The conclusions of this meeting are documented in this LMTF presentation of 16 Feb
2017: https://indico.cern.ch/event/571436/contributions/2479379/attachments/1413751/2169558/ATL￾CMS_crrltnsUpd_R1_WK_6n16Feb17.pdf , where pages 15 & 16 summarize in tabular form the correlations between ATLAS & CMS
luminosity uncertainties applicable to the 2011 and 2012 running periods.
[DAPR-2013-01] ATLAS Collaboration, Luminosity Determination in pp Collisions at sqrt(s) = 8 TeV using the ATLAS Detector at the LHC,
Eur. Phys. J. C 76 (2016) 653, arXiv:1608.03953 [hep-ex].

from beam-separation scans performed during the pPb operation of the LHC in 2013. The dominant uncertainty is associated with non￾factorization effects.
