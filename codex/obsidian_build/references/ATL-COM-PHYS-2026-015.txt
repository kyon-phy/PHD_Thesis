1 Introduction17
Semileptonic decays of𝐵 mesons provide one of the most sensitive probes of lepton flavour universality18
(LFU) in the Standard Model (SM) of particle physics, as well as possible contributions from physics19
‘beyond the SM’ (BSM). In particular, a combination of the BaBar [1, 2], Belle [3–6], Belle-II [7, 8] and20
LHCb[9–12]resultsshowadeviationofabout 3.8𝜎 fromtheSMexpectationusingtheHFLAVCKM202521

2 ATLAS detector59
The ATLAS detector [27, 30] is a multipurpose apparatus with nearly4𝜋 coverage in solid angle.1 It60
consists of an inner tracking detector (ID) inside a2T superconducting solenoid, electromagnetic and61
hadronic calorimeters, and a muon spectrometer. The inner tracking detector covers the pseudorapidity62
range |𝜂|<2.5 . It consists of silicon pixel, silicon microstrip, and transition radiation tracking detectors.63
Lead/liquid-argon (LAr) sampling calorimeters provide electromagnetic (EM) energy measurements with64
high granularity within the region|𝜂|<3.2 . A steel/scintillator-tile hadronic calorimeter covers the central65
pseudorapidity range (|𝜂|<1.7 ). The endcap and forward regions are instrumented with LAr calorimeters66
for EM and hadronic energy measurements up to|𝜂|=4.9 . The muon spectrometer (MS) surrounds the67
calorimeters and is based on three large superconducting air-core toroidal magnets with eight coils each.68
The field integral of the toroids ranges between2.0 and 6.0Tm across most of the detector. The MS69
includes a system of precision tracking chambers up to|𝜂|=2.7 and fast detectors for triggering up to70
|𝜂|=2.4 . A two-level trigger system is used to select events [31]. The first-level trigger is implemented in71
hardware and uses a subset of the detector information to accept events at a rate close to100kHz. This72
is followed by a software-based trigger that reduces the accepted rate of complete events to1.25kHz on73
average in Run 2, depending on the data-taking conditions. A software suite [32] is used in data simulation,74
in the reconstruction and analysis of real and simulated data, in detector operations, and in the trigger and75
data acquisition systems of the experiment.76

10 Conclusion386
This Letter presents the first ATLAS search targeting high-mass𝑈1 leptoquark signals in the𝜏𝜈(+𝑏)387
final state with a hadronically decaying𝜏-lepton and missing transverse momentum. The search uses388
140 fb−1 of data from 13 TeV proton–proton collisions at the LHC. The analysis is designed to probe both389
resonant production and non-resonant𝑡-channel exchange of the leptoquark, covering a wide range of390
coupling scenarios. No significant excess is observed over the SM background. Upper limits on the𝑈1391
vector-leptoquark couplings are set at 95% CL for masses between 1.5 TeV and 3.0 TeV. The results probe392
part of the parameter space at high𝛽23
L values, which is not covered by the previous𝜏𝜏searches.393
