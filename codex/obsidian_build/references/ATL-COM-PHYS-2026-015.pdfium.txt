24 Such extensions of the SM could be described by a leptoquark (LQ) [14–17], a hypothetical scalar or vector
25 boson that couples directly to a lepton and a quark, thereby mediating interactions between the lepton and
quark sectors. The 𝑅(𝐷

28 A comprehensive programme to search for LQ signals with 𝜏-leptons in the final state [21–26] was
29 developed by the ATLAS [27] and CMS [28] experiments at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC). In LQ
30 pair production, the cross-section depends only on the strong coupling constant at leading order, making
31 the searches largely insensitive to the specific choice of the LQ coupling. Signals with LQ masses below
32 approximately 1.6 TeV were excluded by such searches [21, 23]. Searches for singly produced LQs that
33 decay into 𝑏𝜏 were performed in the 𝑏𝜏𝜏 final state, assuming they were produced in association with a
34 𝜏-lepton. If the lepton–quark couplings are large, 𝑡-channel exchange of the LQ can lead to more events with
35 a high-mass final-state than are expected in the SM. ATLAS and CMS have performed such non-resonant

59 2 ATLAS detector
The ATLAS detector [27, 30] is a multipurpose apparatus with nearly 4𝜋 coverage in solid angle.1
60 It
61 consists of an inner tracking detector (ID) inside a 2 T superconducting solenoid, electromagnetic and
62 hadronic calorimeters, and a muon spectrometer. The inner tracking detector covers the pseudorapidity
63 range |𝜂| < 2.5. It consists of silicon pixel, silicon microstrip, and transition radiation tracking detectors.
64 Lead/liquid-argon (LAr) sampling calorimeters provide electromagnetic (EM) energy measurements with
65 high granularity within the region |𝜂| < 3.2. A steel/scintillator-tile hadronic calorimeter covers the central
66 pseudorapidity range (|𝜂| < 1.7). The endcap and forward regions are instrumented with LAr calorimeters
67 for EM and hadronic energy measurements up to |𝜂| = 4.9. The muon spectrometer (MS) surrounds the
68 calorimeters and is based on three large superconducting air-core toroidal magnets with eight coils each.
69 The field integral of the toroids ranges between 2.0 and 6.0 T m across most of the detector. The MS
70 includes a system of precision tracking chambers up to |𝜂| = 2.7 and fast detectors for triggering up to
71 |𝜂| = 2.4. A two-level trigger system is used to select events [31]. The first-level trigger is implemented in
72 hardware and uses a subset of the detector information to accept events at a rate close to 100 kHz. This
73 is followed by a software-based trigger that reduces the accepted rate of complete events to 1.25 kHz on
74 average in Run 2, depending on the data-taking conditions. A software suite [32] is used in data simulation,
75 in the reconstruction and analysis of real and simulated data, in detector operations, and in the trigger and
76 data acquisition systems of the experiment.

386 10 Conclusion
387 This Letter presents the first ATLAS search targeting high-mass 𝑈1 leptoquark signals in the 𝜏𝜈(+𝑏)
388 final state with a hadronically decaying 𝜏-lepton and missing transverse momentum. The search uses
140 fb−1
389 of data from 13 TeV proton–proton collisions at the LHC. The analysis is designed to probe both
390 resonant production and non-resonant 𝑡-channel exchange of the leptoquark, covering a wide range of
391 coupling scenarios. No significant excess is observed over the SM background. Upper limits on the 𝑈1
392 vector-leptoquark couplings are set at 95% CL for masses between 1.5 TeV and 3.0 TeV. The results probe
part of the parameter space at high 𝛽
23
L
393 values, which is not covered by the previous 𝜏𝜏 searches.
