Tasks included in its mandate are described in PMGSubgroupMandates.

Higher Order Cross sections:
When you run on a sample it is necessary to normalise the sample to the right cross-section, if possible this is more
accurate of the cross section obtained during the MC production (MC cross-section). The correction to the cross
section is called k-factor, if a new calculation becomes available it is possible to just modify the k-factor without re￾running the sample. The correct normalisation for each sample (or weight) needs to be evaluated as: MC cross￾section * filter efficiency * k-factor/(Sum of all the weighted events) Here the weights can be the nominal or the
alternative weight for systematic uncertainties/variations.
All the informations on MC cross-section, filter efficiency, k-factor are available through the central page
The list of higher order cross-sections employed is: tt_NNLO single_top_NNLO ttW
The higher order cross-sections for t(t)X processes at 13.6 TeV are still being evaluated.

These uncertainties as for precision top measurements, as top mass, or other
measurements that expect to be sensitive to these effects.
Careful consideration should be given to whether an analysis should be sensitive to a
variation on hdamp which will impact the modelling of the first emission. In some analyses
away from the "bulk" phase-space, this uncertainty should not need to be considered.
It should be noted that by probing shower scale effects using automated shower weights
includes an NLO compensation term which would not be present if generating a
standalone sample with these scales. This will lead to smaller uncertainty than would be
estimated with the other method. However, it is accepted that scale variations between a
factor of 2 and sqrt(2) without an NLO compensation term will be similar to a weighted
variation factor of 2 with an NLO compensation term, so we are not concerned that this
uncertainty is therefore underestimated using the shower weights included in these
uncertainties.
Not all analyses will be affected by this uncertainty, for example analyses measuring the
ttbar differential xs shouldn't be affected. Other analyses sensitive to the shape of the top
(or lepton, jet pt) to extract a parameter (e.g. top mass, ttbar xs) could be affected by the
known mismodelling of this quantity and need to check the effect of including this
additional uncertaninty. In the past the mismodelling of the top pt was covered (by
chance) by the difference between aMcAtNlo+Pythia8 and Powheg+Pythia8, but with the
improvement of the matching uncertainty definition this is not the case anymore.
Instructions on how to perform the reweighting are given below.
ttbar+HF (tt + >=1 b-jet and tt+>=1c) - preliminary
The analyses sensitive to the modelling of tt + Heavy Flavour jets (HF) should use a more robust modelling
systematic model than the one for inclusive ttbar. The exact recommendation depends on the analysis details and
must be discussed with PMG conveners at the initial stage of the analysis. In general, the systematic model should
be similar to the systematic models used in past measurements, e.g. tt+c ( https://cds.cern.ch/record/2789804
arXiv:2409.11305 ) or 4tops 1LOS ( https://cds.cern.ch/record/2722655 arXiv:2106.11683 ) or ttH(bb) Legacy (
https://cds.cern.ch/record/2790108 arXiv:2407.10904 ). The general recommendation is:
1. Divide the inclusive ttbar physics process into categories based on the number of additional heavy-flavour jets.
The three main categories are:
- tt+>=1b - This category should be obtained from the Powheg+Pythia8 simulation of the tt+bb process using 4FS
(in mc20: 603003,603190,603192; in mc23: 601226-601228).
- tt+>=1c - This category should be obtained from the Powheg+Pythia8 simulation of the tt process in 5FS (in mc20:
410470-410472; in mc23: 601229-601230). A particle-level selection should be applied on the number of jets
containing B-hadrons and D-hadrons (see the above references).
- tt+light - The same approach as for tt+>=1c category, just using different particle-level criterion.
Comment 1: Further categorization of tt+>=1b and/or tt+>=1c into subcategories may be necessary, depending on
the analysis details, see for example the ttH(bb) Legacy measurement.
Comment 2: For single-lepton selection of events, ~50% of ttbar events will have a c-jet from the W-boson decay.
This is a potential challenge when categorizing tt+>=1c. In this case, contact the conveners.
2. For each category, use the systematic uncertainties as described in the section for inclusive ttbar
recommendations (ptHard1, Herwig7, scale, ISR, FSR variations). Additionally, for tt+>=1b, the 2-point systematic
dipole vs global recoil is recommended, as it was demonstrated in the ttH(bb) Legacy measurement. For the 2-point
tt+>=1b systematics, use the alternative tt+bb 4FS samples.

Precision recommendations for 𝑡𝑡+𝑊
The ttW process has been studied in the context of ttH(ML) and ttW measurements, where its been found that
electro-weak corrections are non-negligible. Several theoretical progress have been on the calculation to improve
the precision of the sample and the agreement with data. A NLO sample up to 2 additional jet emissions, including
these EW contributions, using the FxFx algorithm has been made in FxFx EWK jets . The most precise theory
calculation is aNNLO .
Even with these precise reccomendations it is not trivial to normalise the sample because separate NLO EWK
components can be added and the normalisation depends on the samples used. We reccomend 2 approaches,
depending on the analysis.
ttW is the major background or the signal:
2 separate samples: ttW (QCD+EWK lead) and ttW (EWK-sub)
Normalise the first to (QCD+EWK lead) from aNNLO and the second to EWK-sub from FxFx.
ttW is a small background:
Just take ttW(QCD) sample and apply (EWK-lead+EWK-sub) normalisation correction
See also this presentation for the case-by-case normalisation proposed for Run2 analyses.
Sherpa allows the use of different MC weights to add the EW contributions. This AQP details the usage of these
weights and maps them to the diagrams that are added by each of them; it also explains in detail the internal
variations (see table above) available in Sherpa that should be used if ttW is your signal or a major background.
These EW weights may be added using two different schemes, additive or multiplicative, and their impact has been
studied here (Appendix L). The difference was found to be small so the additive scheme was used for the paper.
For Sherpa, the EW-lead contributions can be added via reweighting, using the *ASSEWLO1 MC weight (but
keeping the sum of weights of the nominal MC weight). Since Run3 is currently lacking a dedicated ttW EWK-sub
sample, its contribution can be estimated using the Sherpa internal weight *ASSEWLO1LO2LO3 and normalised to
the aNNLO prediction.

ttGamma
These processes need to be evaluated combining an NLO sample where the gamma is produced during production
and a LO sample where the photon is emitted in the decay. Uncertainties need to be evaluated for each sample.
Inclusive samples (as NLO 𝑡𝑡) also includes the radiation of gluons performed by the showering algorithm, to avoid
double counting a specific technique needs to be applied. Detailes are explained Section3 .
Lineshape uncertainty
All our ttbar generators, excluding bb4l and NLO-dec (under development), consider the top-quarks on shell in
production and then include approximations to recover the shape of the top mass with a proper width and high off￾shell behaviours. This makes, expecially the highly off-shell region, unreliable. A perfect solution would be to use
bb4l, where no approximations to the lineshape are employed. The lineshape uncertainty targets this ambiguity of
our "standard" generators, aiming to compare the 2 existing implementations of the smearing and off-shell
corrections: Powheg and Madspin.
Studies show that the agreement between our nominal and bb4l is good in the region around the top-quark mass,
see bb4l PUB note , so we reccomend this uncertainty only for analayses targeting the tails of the top mass or
mttbar distribution. The discussion on this uncertainty is still open so the conclusion and application suggestions
may change.
Color Reconnection
Pythia8 provides a number of colour reconnection models . Studies were performed in ATLAS where three new
models were tested by taking A14, and switching to CR0, CR1 or CR2 and then performing a returning of
parameters applicable to colour reconnection (see ATL-PHYS-PUB-2017-008 ). Analyses which need to estimate
the impact of changing the colour-reconnection model should take the largest of these variations as uncertainty. If
the two variations go in opposite direction, the analysis appears to be very senstitive to this variation and taking the
largest doesn't guarantee the most conservative approach (e.g. fit) analyses should consider both variations.
A new sample has been generated with an additional variation in the color reconnection scheme (ERD On samples).
While the default Pythia8 settings do not allow to the decay products to reconnect in these samples the
reconnection is enabled and the range for reconnection is also enlarged. These samples are more similar to
Herwig7 setting, compared to the nominal Pythia8. These samples are currently available only for testing purpose,
they are not included in any reccomendation.
Recoil to Top uncertainty
The modelling of the parton shower of the b-quarks in 𝑡 → 𝑊 𝑏 is affected by the gluon recoil scheme. The default
Run2 samples employs the recoiling against b-quarks (recoilToColor=ON in the pythia8 settings), independently on
the color connection of the emitted gluon. This setting changes the modelling of second and subsequent gluon
emission from quarks produced by coloured resonance decays, such as the 𝑏-quark in a 𝑡 → 𝑊 𝑏 process (but it has
no impact for example on 𝑍 → 𝑏𝑏¯ decays), practically deciding how the momentum is re-arranged between the W
and the b. Pythia8 developer suggetsed that the recoilToColor scheme cut large-angle radiation off the b-quark and
a better scheme would be the recoilToTop, where the top-quark itself works as recoiler for the second and
subsequent emissions. This option give wider-angle gluon radiation, resulting in energy deposits that do not get
clustered into the 𝑏-jet, and lower gluon-energy emission, altering the modelling of the 𝑏-quark fragmentation and
hardening the 𝑏-hadron momenta. This text is largely taken by the first ATLAS analysis using this reccomendation:
SMT_mass_measurement .

The recoil to top scheme should, currently, be taken as an uncertainty with respect to the nominal recoil scheme.
This uncertainty is currently enlarged by the lack of a dedicated tuning that takes into account the recoilToTop
scheme. The recoilToTop option is implemented in a dedicated userhook provided by the Pythia8 authors to ATLAS
in Pythia versions < 8.309 and available in more recent versions changing the parameter (
TimeShower:recoilStrategyRF )
Please note that these variations are only available for the 𝑡𝑡 and t-channel process. The t-channel sample is mainly
used by the t-channel top-mass analysis. If other top-processes are considered for measuring the top-quark mass
please get in contact as some additional samples for other processes may be needed.
Reweighting top-pair events to NNLO prediction
There are some issues relating to top modelling which are inextricably linked to the precision of our MC calculations
(eg top pT mismodelling). Higher order corrections improves this mismodelling, but reweight to these predictions is
complicated and using only one observables (e.g. top pT) will typically result in destroying the precision of other
differential variables due to cross-correlations. Using a recursive approach to the reweighting, including multiple
variables, partially solves this issue. Tools for the reweighting have been developed by member of the top group:
TTBarNNLOReweighter (Original) package - https://gitlab.cern.ch/lserkin/TTbarNNLOReweighter
TTBarNNLOReweighter (Recursive) package - https://gitlab.cern.ch/pinamont/TTbarNNLOReweighter
They can also be adapted to take as input a custom higher order calculation obtained with Matrix or HighTEA , this
is useful for 13.6 energies.
It is possible to also call this tool inside a rivet routine, more information and instructions on how to do so are shown
here: J.Jamieson
Searches may find useful to use the reweighted sample as nominal, in this case all the uncertainties are to be
evaluated coherently, an example of this kind of approach can be find here: 𝑡𝑡 resonance
𝑡𝑡/𝑡𝑊/𝑊𝑊𝑏𝑏 Interference
𝑡𝑊 diagrams at NLO QCD clearly overlap with LO 𝑡𝑡 diagrams. To account for this overlap, the 𝑡𝑊 process is
generated removing these diagrams to allow a merging which does not double-count, but it is possible to be in a
region of phase-space which becomes sensitive to the interference between these processes in the resonant region
where t+Wb is produced with m(Wb) = m(top). The nominal Powheg+Pythia8 𝑡𝑊 sample uses the diagram removal
scheme (DR) which removes the overlap at the cross-section level and keeps the interference in the prediction.
Alternative treatments are possible on the interference, the current reccomendation is the comparison with DS. In
case an analysis would like to employ DS as nominal please contact us. The usage of dynamic scales in general
reduces this uncertainty.
The correct approach to include the interference, that also entirely remove this uncertainty, would be to consider the
𝑡𝑡 and 𝑡𝑊 as a single process and so having a simulation of WbWb. We have currently a sample produced using
bb4l that simulate the full WbWb process. The dileptonic sample is currently available and the semileptonic one will
be ready soon (together with a newer version for the dileptonic). A complete set of uncertainties around this sample
is being developed in one of the ongoing projects.
