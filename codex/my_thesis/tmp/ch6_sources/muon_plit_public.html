Muon isolation with Machine Learning algorithms with the ATLAS detector

The Prompt Lepton Isolation Tagger (PLIT) is an isolation algorithm using a transformer neural network architecture to separate prompt leptons (electrons and muons) arising in decays of electroweak $W$ and $Z$ bosons from non-prompt leptons produced in semi-leptonic decays of $b$- and $c$ hadrons. It plays a crucial role in various multi-lepton final states, by effectively suppressing the presence of non-prompt leptons. This document presents preliminary results on the the performance of the algorithm using simulated events produced with setups corresponding to the Run 2 (2015--2018), as well as the partial Run 3 (2022 and 2023) data acquisition configurations of ATLAS. The PLIT algorithm is based on the same multi-task transformer neural network architecture used in ATLAS for the identification of jets originating from heavy-flavour quarks ATL-PHYS-PUB-2022-027 , ATL-PHYS-PUB-2023-021 . It uses features from the lepton, features from tracks within a cone of $\Delta R < 0.4$ of the lepton, and some features which are computed with respect to the variable-radius track jet ATL-PHYS-PUB-2017-010 closest in $\Delta R$ with $\Delta R < 0.4$ to the lepton. A final PLIT discriminant score is evaluated to discriminate signal prompt muons from background non-prompt muons. The training is performed on 40 million muons from a mixture of samples of simulated $t\overline{t}$ events with centre of mass energies of 13 and 13.6 TeV generated at next-to-leading order (NLO) in the quantum chromodynamics (QCD) coupling constant $\alpha_s$ with the Powheg Box v2 event generator ( JHEP 06 (2010) 043 ) interfaced with Pythia 8.230 ( Comput. Phys. Commun. 191 (2015) 159 ), using a full detector simulation ( Eur. Phys. J. C 70 (2010) 823 ) based on Geant4 ( Nucl. Instrum. Meth. A 506 (2003) 250 ) and the EvtGen 1.2.0 program ( Nucl. Instrum. Meth. A 462 (2001) 152 ) for the modelling of heavy-flavour hadron decays. The muons in the training dataset are required to pass the Medium identification working point (Eur. Phys. J. C 81 (2021) 578), satisfy ($p_{T} > $ 10 GeV and $|\eta| < 2.5$), and an associated track with impact parameters consistent with the primary vertex by requiring ($d_{0} / sigma(d_{0}) < 3$ and $(z_{0} /sin \theta < 0.5$ mm). The supervised training is based on preserved generator-level information to match the track of each reconstructed lepton candidate to a suitable stable particle produced by the generator or the Geant4 simulation. Muons in this dataset are labelled as prompt if they are matched to a generator-level muon originating from the decay of a top quark or a $W$ boson. They are labelled non-prompt otherwise. The training sample is prepared with a resampling procedure to ensure a proportion of 3:1 and equal $p_{\textrm{T}}$ and $\eta$ distributions between prompt muons and non-prompt muons.

Figure 01


The network architecture of the Prompt Lepton Isolation Tagger (PLIT). The PLIT algorithm is based on the same multi-task transformer architecture used in ATLAS for the identification of jets originating from heavy-flavour quarks ATL-PHYS-PUB-2022-027 , ATL-PHYS-PUB-2023-021 . First, input features associated with the lepton are concatenated with features of tracks within a cone of $\Delta R < 0.4$ of the lepton. Next, an initial embedding to a representation space is performed with a Deep Sets (Deep Sets, 2017 ) network without using the aggregation over the output track representations. The track representations are fed into a transformer encoder NormFormer 2021 . The output representation of each track is then combined to form a global representation of the lepton to be used for classification. This global representation is formed by a weighted sum over the track representations, where the attention weights for the sum are learned during training. In addition to the primary lepton classification task, PLIT also has auxiliary training objectives to classify the track origin and grouping tracks into common vertices. Each of the tasks consists of three hidden layers containing 128, 64 and 32 neurons respectively. These auxiliary tasks enhance the sensitivity to leptons from semi-leptonic $B$-decays which often have decay secondary vertices.
