6.4.3 Isolation
Muons from heavy-flavour decays are often accompanied by other particles inside a jet. They
are suppressed using track and calorimeter isolation, defined by the surrounding activity after
excluding the muon’s own contribution [2]. Baseline muons are required to pass Loose_VarRad
isolation, and signal muons must additionally satisfy Tight_VarRad. Both working points use
a track-cone radius that decreases as the muon pT increases and a fixed calorimeter cone of
∆R = 0.2. The track and calorimeter isolation sums must be below 15% and 30% of the muon pT
for Loose_VarRad, respectively. These limits are reduced to 4% and 15% for Tight_VarRad,
allowing less activity around the muon and rejecting more muons produced inside jets [2].
The typical prompt-muon isolation efficiencies are about 95% for Loose_VarRad and 86% for
Tight_VarRad [8].
6.4.4 Calibration
The muon momentum scale and resolution are calibrated using resonance decays, e.g., Z →
µµ and J/ψ → µµ [9]. The reconstructed mass distributions constrain biases in the muon
momentum measurement. The efficiency scale factors are measured with tag-and-probe samples
as for electrons and applied to simulated event weights for the selected working points [2].
