1 Introduction18
Semileptonic decays of𝐵 mesons provide one of the most sensitive probes of lepton flavour universality19
in the Standard Model (SM) of particle physics, as well as possible contributions from physics beyond20
the SM. In particular, a combination of the BaBar [1, 2], Belle [3–6], Belle-II [7, 8] and LHCb [9–12]21
results reported about a3.8𝜎 deviation from the SM expectation on the ratio of the branching fractions22
𝑅(𝐷 (∗) )=B (𝐵→𝐷 (∗) 𝜏𝜈)/B (𝐵→𝐷 (∗) ℓ𝜈), where ℓ=𝑒, 𝜇 (ℓ=𝜇 for LHCb) [13], motivating23
scenarios with new interactions in the𝑏→𝑐𝜏𝜈 transition. Such extensions to the SM [14–16] could be24
described by a leptoquark (LQ), a hypothetical scalar or vector boson that couples directly to a lepton25
and a quark, thereby mediating interactions between the lepton and quark sectors. This search focuses26
on the production of a vector leptoquark𝑈1 with quantum numbers(3,1,2/3) , which correspond to the27
colour, weak, and hypercharge representations, respectively. The𝑅(𝐷 (∗) ) anomaly requires significant28
contributions from both𝑏𝜏and𝑐𝜈couplings of a LQ.29
A comprehensive programme to search for LQ signals with𝜏 leptons in the final state has been developed30
by the ATLAS and CMS experiments [17–22] at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC). In LQ pair production,31
thecrosssectiondependsonlyonthestrongcouplingconstantatleadingorder, makingthesearcheslargely32
insensitivetothespecificchoiceoftheLQcoupling. SignalswithLQmassesbelowapproximately1.6 TeV33
wereexcludedbysuchsearches. SearchesforsingleproductionofaLQdecayinginto 𝑏𝜏 wereperformedin34
the 𝑏𝜏𝜏 final state, assuming it was produced in association with a𝜏 lepton. If the lepton-quark couplings35
are large,𝑡-channel exchange of the LQ can lead to an excess of events over the SM expectation in the36
high-mass region of the final-state system. ATLAS and CMS have performed such non-resonant searches37
for LQ signals in the𝜏𝜏 final state. For the𝑈1 LQ model, the region with𝜆𝑏𝜏 /𝑚U1 >∼1TeV −1 has been38
excluded by ATLAS [19], where𝜆𝑏𝜏 =𝑔 𝑈/

Previous ATLAS and CMS searches in𝜏𝜏(+𝑏) final states mentioned above assumed that the signal rate42
depends only on the𝑏𝜏 coupling, because the𝑐𝜈 coupling contributes only through subleading effects.43
In contrast, this is the first ATLAS analysis targeting the𝜏𝜈(+𝑏) final state. Figure 1 shows illustrative44
Feynman diagrams for the signals targeted by this analysis. A search for leptoquarks is performed in45
events with a hadronically-decaying𝜏 lepton (𝜏had) with high transverse momentum (𝑝T) and large missing46
transverse momentum. The analysis is categorised according to the presence or absence of a jet containing47
𝑏-hadrons (𝑏-jet) and the transverse momentum range of the jet. The categories with𝑏-jet are sensitive to48
the product of the𝑏𝜏 and 𝑐𝜈 couplings, directly relevant to the𝑅(𝐷 (∗) ) anomaly, while categories without49

CMS in the𝜏𝜈final state [24] was performed without𝑏-jet requirements.54

2 ATLAS detector55
The ATLAS detector [25, 26] is a multipurpose apparatus with nearly4𝜋 coverage in solid angle.1 It56
consists of an inner tracking detector (ID) inside a2T superconducting solenoid magnet, electromagnetic57
and hadronic calorimeters, and a muon spectrometer. The inner tracking detector covers the pseudorapidity58
range |𝜂|<2.5 . It consists of silicon pixel, silicon microstrip, and transition radiation tracking detectors.59
Lead/liquid-argon (LAr) sampling calorimeters provide electromagnetic (EM) energy measurements with60
high granularity within the region|𝜂|<3.2 . A steel/scintillator-tile hadronic calorimeter covers the central61
pseudorapidity range (|𝜂|<1.7 ). The endcap and forward regions are instrumented with LAr calorimeters62
for EM and hadronic energy measurements up to|𝜂|=4.9 . The muon spectrometer (MS) surrounds the63
calorimeters and is based on three large superconducting air-core toroidal magnets with eight coils each.64
The field integral of the toroids ranges between2.0 and 6.0Tm across most of the detector. The MS65
includes a system of precision tracking chambers up to|𝜂|=2.7 and fast detectors for triggering up to66
|𝜂|=2.4 . A two-level trigger system was used to select events [27]. The first-level trigger is implemented67
in hardware and uses a subset of the detector information to accept events at a rate close to100kHz. This68
is followed by a software-based trigger that reduces the accepted rate of complete events to1.25kHz on69
average in Run 2, depending on the data-taking conditions. A software suite [28] is used in data simulation,70
in the reconstruction and analysis of real and simulated data, in detector operations, and in the trigger and71
data acquisition systems of the experiment.72

10 Conclusion359
This letter has presented a first search for high-mass leptoquark signals with ATLAS, targeting final states360
with a hadronically-decaying𝜏 lepton and missing transverse momentum. The analysis is designed to361
