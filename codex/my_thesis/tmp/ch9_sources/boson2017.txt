1 Introduction

The production of electroweak vector bosons (W and Z/γ∗ , which will be indicated in the remainder of
this document as V) is a very important process at the LHC. Measurements of such processes allow to
gain a deeper understanding of pQCD and to test the currently available predictions. These analyses
are generally carried out looking at final states containing electrons or muons, exploiting relatively clean
experimental signatures. With the higher centre-of-mass energy as well as the increased luminosity ex-
pected for the LHC Run 2, rarer processes like the electroweak production of vector bosons in association
with two jets, including vector boson fusion processes, become more accessible as well.
Furthermore, V + jets production is also a major background to a large number of analyses, such as top-
quark, Higgs-boson measurements, and searches for new physics phenomena. An accurate modelling of
the data is therefore necessary in this type of analyses, and often an accuracy beyond the calculated order
is desired.
Typical Monte Carlo (MC) generators that are used to generate V + jets events combine multi-leg matrix
elements at LO [1–3] or even NLO [4] with the parton shower in order to include additional parton
emissions at matrix-element accuracy. Merging algorithms are used to avoid double counting the final-
state configurations that are also produced by the parton shower. It is clear that the production of reliable
and high-statistics Monte Carlo (MC) generator samples is a key ingredient at multi-purpose hadron
collider experiments. In the following sections, the main generators used in the ATLAS experiment [5]
during Run 2 will be discussed and studied in detail.
In this note, comparisons of different generator predictions for V + jets processes are made at centre-
of-mass energies of 8 and 13 TeV, including comparisons to recent ATLAS measurements. The studies
presented follow naturally from the ones presented in [6], which focussed on comparisons in regions of
phase space used mainly in Run 1 analyses. For this note, comparisons are made in regions of phase space
more suitable for Run 2 analyses, with a particular focus on the production of vector bosons in association
with heavy-flavour jets (also in the context of the search for Higgs decays to a b-quark pair in the same
final state) and a study of systematic uncertainties for electroweak Vjj production is also performed. The
scope of this note is twofold; the aim is to document the baseline samples used by ATLAS analyses in
Run 2, whilst also providing insight into the differences between these samples in regions of phase space
relevant to the LHC analyses, including the size of systematic uncertainties on the theoretical modelling.
In addition, preliminary studies on a specific MG5_aMC+Py8 CKKW-L tune, with rapidity-ordering
shower are compared to simpler Pythia8-only tunes.
Throughout this note, V + jets predictions are only shown for the Z/γ∗ → e+ e− and W ± → e± ν channel
(with the exception of the study dedicated to the V H(H → bb̄) background studies in Section 5.3) and
the ATLAS coordinate system is adopted, which consists of a right-handed system with the z-direction
defined by the beam direction, and x pointing towards the centre of the LHC ring. The polar angle
θ is defined relative to the z axis, and pseudorapidity η = −ln(tan(θ/2)), with the transverse direction
corresponding to θ = π/2, η = 0. Transverse momentum, pT , is defined as the component of momentum
in the transverse direction. The polar angle, φ, is defined in the x − y plane. Angular separation is defined
by ∆R = ∆φ + ∆η2 . Rapidity is defined by y = 0.5 × (E + pz )/(E − pz ), where E is the total energy,

2 Baseline generators

In the following paragraphs, the setup of the samples used in this note for the different set of comparisons
is described. Sherpa and MG5_aMC@NLO are the two most commonly used V + jets generators for
ATLAS Run 2 analyses using the 2015 and 2016 datasets. The focus is towards the usage of modern
generator predictions, in particular the development of a NLO-merged setups.


2.1 Sherpa

Sherpa [7] is a parton shower Monte Carlo generator simulating additional hard parton emissions [8]
that are matched to a parton shower based on Catani–Seymour subtraction terms [9]. The merging of
multi-parton matrix elements with the parton shower is achieved using an improved CKKW matching
procedure [1, 2], which is extended to next-to-leading order (NLO) accuracy using the MEPS@NLO
prescription [4]. Virtual corrections for matrix elements at NLO accuracy are provided by OpenLoops [10]
using the Collier tensor integral reduction library [11]. Sherpa v2.2 is used as a baseline using parton
density functions (PDFs) provided by the NNPDF3.0nnlo set [12] as well as the dedicated set of tuned
parton-shower parameters developed by the Sherpa authors for this version.
Matrix elements for the `+ `− + jets and `ν + jets final states (with ` = e, µ, τ) have been generated
using Sherpa v2.2 with up to two additional parton emissions at NLO accuracy and up to four additional
parton emissions at LO accuracy. The matrix elements are merged with the Sherpa parton shower using
the MEPS@NLO prescription using the five-flavour-number scheme (5FNS) with a matching scale of
20 GeV. The invariant mass of the dilepton system for the `+ `− + jets final states is required to be at least
40 GeV.
Each final state is sliced in the variable max HT , pT (V) at the parton level, where the boson transverse
                                                          
momentum, pT (V), is worked out from the two parton-level leptons and the parton-level HT is given by
the scalar pT sum of all parton-level jets with pT > 20 GeV. Here, parton-level jets are constructed from
all remaining matrix-element-level partons (excluding the two leptons) using the anti-kt algorithm [13]
with a jet-radius parameter 0.4. Below max HT , pT (V) 500 GeV, the slices are also filtered according to
                                                        
their b-hadron and c-hadron content at the particle level.
Separate samples are generated for electroweak Vjj production, with two additional electroweak couplings
at tree level. Matrix elements for the `+ `− j j and `ν j j final states have been generated using Sherpa v2.2
with up to two additional parton emissions at LO accuracy beyond the first two jets. At tree level, this
gives rise to a gauge-invariant set of seven V j j diagrams that involve a t-channel exchange of a heavy
gauge boson using three electroweak couplings (and an additional one for the decay of the boson). The
matrix elements are merged with the Sherpa parton shower using the MEPS@LO prescription with a
matching scale of 15 GeV. The invariant mass of the dilepton system for the `+ `− j j final states is required
to be at least 40 GeV.
Contributions from top-quark interactions are avoided by generating matrix elements without bottom
quarks for the hard-scattering process. The Min_N_TChannels option has been activated in order to
remove contributions from diboson interactions that do not contain a t-channel exchange of a heavy
gauge boson. The V j j samples are produced using the Gµ scheme, making use of the tree-level relations
between the electroweak input parameters.

2.2 MadGraph5_aMC@NLO using CKKW-L

Matrix elements for V+ up to 4 partons at LO accuracy are produced using MadGraph5_aMC@NLO v2.2.2 [16]
interfaced with Pythia v8.186 [17] for the modelling of the parton shower and underlying event. The
CKKW-L matching and merging procedure is applied, with a merging scale of 30 GeV. This setup is here-
after referred to as MG5_aMC@NLO CKKW-L. The renormalisation and factorisation scales are set to
the MadGraph5_aMC@NLO default values, based on a clustering of the event. The NNPDF3.0nlo PDF
set is used, with αS = 0.118. The A14 tune [18] of Pythia 8 is used, and the EvtGen v1.2.0 program [19]
is used for properties of the bottom and charm hadron decays. MadGraph5_aMC@NLO performs a 5-
flavour (5FNS) calculation with massless b- and c-quarks in the matrix element, and massive quarks in
the Pythia 8 shower. Samples produced with the described setup, sometimes referred as MG5_aMC+Py8
CKKW-L B in agreement with the definition of [6], corresponds to the main MG5_aMC+Py8 CKKW-
L samples used for the studies reported in this note. A second set of samples, which use instead the
NNPDF2.3lo PDF set [20] with αS = 0.13, is also available and referred as MG5_aMC+Py8 CKKW-L
A, in agreement with the definition of [6].


2.3 MadGraph5_aMC@NLO using FxFx

Samples have also been generated using the MadGraph5_aMC@NLO program to generate matrix ele-
ments for V + 0, 1 and 2 partons to NLO accuracy. The showering and subsequent hadronisation has
been performed using Pythia 8.210 with the A14 tune, using the NNPDF2.3lo PDF set with α s = 0.13.
The different jet multiplicities are merged using the FxFx prescription [21] implemented in the Mad-
Graph5_aMC@NLO program (version 2.3.3 is used here).
MG5_aMC@NLO performs a 5FNS calculation with massless b- and c-quarks in the matrix element, and
massive quarks in the Pythia8 shower. The PDF input set in use for event generation is NNPDF2.3nlo
PDF set with αS = 0.119 and the samples have been generated with additional weights for the PDF
replicas as well as scale variations of the renormalisation and the factorisation scales [22].
The impact of various merging scales (µQ ) has been studied, analysing three different values: 20 GeV
(down variation), 25 GeV (nominal value) and 50 GeV (up variation). At the event-generation level, the

value of the jet pseudorapity (jet |η|). The samples have been generated using LHAPDF-6.1.5 [23] and
FastJet-3.1.0 [24].

2.4 Powheg MiNLO

Predictions from Powheg MiNLO [25–27] interfaced to Pythia 8.210 [17] with the AZNLO tune [28]
were obtained to produce V + jets events, and will be referred to in the remainder of the note as Powheg
MiNLO+Pythia8. The EvtGen v1.2.0 program [19] is used as an afterburner to better simulate the decays
of bottom and charm hadrons. The Photos++ 3.61 program [29, 30] is interfaced to Pythia 8 to accurately
decribe the QED final state radiation, and the emission of lepton pairs is activated in this setup. The PDF
set used in Powheg is CT14nnlo [31] whereas the PDF set used in the parton shower is the CTEQ6 L1 [32]
leading order set.
MiNLO is an improvement of the CKKW matching procedure to reach NLO accuracy for boson produc-
tion in association one jet, and smoothly merges to NLO single boson production at low boson pT , using
no external merging scale. NLO contributions from the CKKW Sudakov form factors are subtracted to
the virtual corrections provided by PowhegBox. Thanks to the optimal scale and to the Sudakov form
factors, the Powheg MiNLO samples do not need any Born suppression cut, and their cross-sections are
well-behaved at small boson pT , contrary to fixed order predictions that diverge in this kinematic phase
space.


2.5 ALPGEN

Predictions for V + jets production at 13 TeV have also been produced using the LO matrix-element
generator ALPGEN v2.14 [33] interfaced to Pythia v6.426 [34] to model the parton shower, hereafter
referred to as ALPGEN+Pythia6. The parton-shower tune Perugia2011C [35] and the CTEQ6L1 PDF
set [32] have been used. Up to five additional partons are modelled by the matrix elements, merged with
the MLM prescription [36] with a matching scale of 20 GeV. Different V plus N-partons multiplicities,
with N ranging from 0 to 5, are generated in separate samples and, as no distinction is made about the
flavour of the parton used in the calculation, the samples are sometimes referred to as “inclusive”.
The predictions follow a four-flavour-number-scheme (4FNS) for the production of heavy-flavour jets.
Explicit matrix element evaluation, with massive treatment of the heavy-flavour quarks, is also possible
for the following processes (dubbed V + hf):

                                  Z + bb̄, Z + cc̄, W + c, W + cc̄, W + bb̄,                               (1)

and, again, a total of up to five partons (generic plus heavy-flavour) can be considered in the calculation.
Each of the V + hf sample has been produced separately, with up to five partons, and a heavy-flavour
overlap removal (HFOR) procedure is used combine the “inclusive” and V + hf samples. The HFOR is
needed to avoid double counting of phase space between different samples because heavy-flavour quarks
are both produced by the gluon splitting in the parton shower and by the explicit matrix-element calcula-
tions. The HFOR criteria require the removal of an event if it contains two heavy-flavour quarks arising
from the PS with ∆R(q, q̄) > 0.4 as well as events which contain two heavy-flavour quarks arising from
ME and with ∆R(q, q̄) < 0.4. The pairing of quarks is performed only between same flavours (b or c) and
between particle/anti-particle pairs. Quarks produced by multi-parton interactions or originating directly
from PDFs are not included in the HFOR procedure. The HFOR procedure reject from about 5% to 30%
of the generated events, depending if the analysed sample has lower or larger parton multiplicity or on
the considered parton flavour.




                                                      5
The HFOR procedure is applied analysing the HepMC record of each event during a pre-processing stage,
before the evaluation of any other kinematic requirement. The predicted cross section of each separate
sample is then scaled down to take into account the removed fraction of events. The total cross section
predicted by the combination of all the samples is in agreement within 1% with the prediction of the
“inclusive” only samples. Several kinematic variables are affected by the combination of the “inclusive”
and V + hf samples, however a net effect immediately visible is an enhancement of the differential cross
section for jet multiplicities ≥ 1.

3 Rivet routines for comparison

Particle-level comparisons of different generator setups are made using Rivet v2.5.3 [38] using the fol-
lowing analyses:
   • ATLAS 13 TeV analysis of Z+ jets production using an integrated luminosity of 3.16 fb−1 [39] (not
     yet published in Rivet). A pair of opposite-charge dressed leptons, with pT > 25 GeV and |η| < 2.5
     are required, with dilepton mass between 66 and 116 GeV. Jets are constructed using the anti-kt
     algorithm [13] using a jet-radius parameter of 0.4, and required to have pT > 30 GeV, |y| < 2.5 and
     not to overlap with the selected leptons (∆R( j, `) > 0.4).
   • ATLAS 8 TeV measurement of the splitting scales (ATLAS_2017_I1589844) occurring in the kt
     algorithm [40, 41] using 20.2 fb−1 [42]. In the electron channel, exactly two dressed electrons are
     selected with pT > 25 GeVand |η| < 2.47, excluding the transition region between the ATLAS
     barrel and end cap electromagnetic calorimeters (1.37 < |η| < 1.52). The dilepton invariant mass
     is required to be within 71 GeV < m`` < 111 GeV. All charged final-state particles with pT >
     400 MeVand |η| < 2.5, excluding the tracks of the two selected electrons, are then passed through
     the kt algorithm and its splitting scales are measured for jet-radius parameters at R = 0.4 as well as
     R = 1.0.
   • Analysis of V + b(b) events in different phase space regions: an inclusive region, aimed at the
     differential measurement of W and Z production in association with heavy-flavour jets, a high-pT
     selection, aimed at understanding this type of events when the two b-jets are almost collinear and
     have a very high boost as well as an intermediate region, aimed at describing the main irreducible
     background of the V H(H → bb̄) process. Jets originating from b-quarks are identified by the
     presence of a weakly decaying b-hadron with pT > 5 GeV and decay products within the area of
     the jet (ghost-matching). This procedure mimics a b-tagging algorithm with efficiency of 100%,
     therefore such jet selection is often referred as b-tagging in the following. Further details about the
     mentioned analyses will be given in the relevant comparison sections.
   • Analysis of electroweak Wjj production, based on the ATLAS 8 TeV measurement [43]. One lepton
     with pT > 25 GeV and |η| < 2.5 is required, together with a missing energy larger than 20 GeV. The
     mT is required to be larger than 40 GeVand the two associated jets to have a transverse momentum
     of 80 and 60 GeV, respectively for the leading and subleading one, and to be within 4.4 in rapidity.
     Additional topological cuts are applied to the jet pair, requiring their invariant mass (m j j ) to be
     larger than 500 GeV, and ∆y j j > 2 and ∆R j` > 0.3.
   • ATLAS 8 TeV measurement of electroweak dijet production in association with a Z boson (Zjj) [44].
     The corresponding routine (ATLAS_2014_I1279489) has been modified and is used to compare the
     13 TeV samples (without data). The selection remains the same, but the binning has been adjusted
     to suit the reach of the 13 TeV predictions.
   • Analysis of W + jets final states in the W → eν channel. This routine was initially designed
     for a centre-of-mass energy of 8 TeV, but is used in the context of this note to study the 13 TeV
     predictions. An electron is selected and needs to be within |η| < 2.47 and have pT > 25 GeV. A
     symmetric cut on the missing energy at 25 GeV is applied, and the transverse mass is required to be
     above 40 GeV. Only jets with pT > 30 GeV and |y| < 4.4 are considered in the analysis.

Figures 7–10 show generator comparisons of W+jets predictions from Sherpa 2.2 and

Generator uncertainties have been estimated using Sherpa 2.2 and are indicated by the orange band in
the ratio panels. The band is constructed from the statistical uncertainty of the Sherpa predictions, the

the renormalisation and factorisation scales. The various uncertainty components are added in quadrature
to give the total uncertainty shown.
In Figure 7, it can be seen that the uncertainty for the MEPS@NLO setup is at the few-percent level
for the first couple of jet bins that have NLO accuracy, then rises to about 20 % for the LO-accurate jet
multiplicities and finally becomes very large for high jet multiplicities that are dominated by the parton
shower.

distributions shown in Figure 9 are due to very large event weights that can occur if the differential cross
section of a phase-space point in the Monte Carlo integration exceeds the maximum weight estimated
during the phase-space optimisation.

5 W and Z boson plus heavy-flavour jets

The V + b(b) production processes are an extremely interesting benchmark for MC simulations because
the predicted cross-section rates and distributions give an insight on both the structure of the proton and
on the gluon splitting into a pair of b-quarks. The V + b(b) processes are also one of the largest and most
difficult to model backgrounds for searches at hadron colliders. This is reflected particularly in the case
where the pT (V) is very large, and in the case of the search for the SM V H(H → bb̄) process, where they
constitute the main irreducible background, and the uncertainty in their predictions largely affects the
result. These aspects will be treated in the following, starting by looking at important kinematic variables
for the V + b(b) process measurements, then studying the modelling in case of a large boost of the di-b-jet
system, and subsequently in the context of a SM V H(H → bb̄) analysis, where it is often hard to identify
in data a control region pure enough in V + b(b) production, making it necessary to carefully understand
the available predictions.
In this section the V+jets generators described in Section 2 are compared, at a centre-of-mass energy of
13 TeV, using custom Rivet routines aimed at selecting final states with b-jets, specifically:
   b. W(→ `ν) + b(b)
   a. Z(→ ``) + b(b)
   c. Z(→ νν) + b(b).
For all the three mentioned processes we examined the case of b-jets reconstructed using the anti-kt
algorithm, with a radius parameter R of 0.4. For the second process, it is examined also the case of one
single anti-kt jet of R = 1 (large-radius jet) having two associated b-quarks, and the pT of the large-radius
jet is taken to be larger than 250 GeV.
Predictions for V + b(b) processes in the 5FNS are available using the Sherpa 2.2 and MG5_aMC+Py8
CKKW-L samples, while prediction in the 4FNS are available using the Alpgen samples.

5.3 Relevant variables for the VH(H → b b̄) analysis

As mentioned above, the V + b(b) process is one of the main irreducible backgrounds for in the V H(H →
bb̄) search. Moreover, in such a search it is often difficult to identify a pure enough control region where
constrains or information on this background process could be extracted. For this reason, the knowledge
of the modelling of the V + b(b) processes is crucial, and the uncertainty coming from the predictions is
one of the largest affecting the Higgs signal extraction, as can be observed in the latest ATLAS results on
V H(H → bb̄) [46]. In the following, studies aimed at understanding the impact of the V + b(b) modelling
in the specific V H analysis-regions are reported.
In the analysis, jets are reconstructed using the anti-kt clustering algorithm with a jet-radius parameter of
R = 0.4. The jet transverse momentum is required to be greater than 20 GeVfor central jets (|η| < 2.5) and




                                                                                  22
greater than 30 GeVfor forward jets (2.5 < |η| < 5), and the leading jet is required to have a transverse
momentum larger than 45 GeV. Selected central jets are labelled according to their flavour with the
following procedure:
   1. if a b-hadron is found within a cone of ∆R = 0.3 centered along the jet axis (considering all particles
      that belong to the jet constituents and their ancestors in the MC truth record) the jet is b-labelled;
   2. if a jet is not b-labelled, and a c-hadron is found within a cone of ∆R = 0.3 centered along the jet
      axis the jet is c-labelled;
   3. if a jet is not b-labelled neither c-labelled, it is then labelled as light-flavour (l-labelled).
To select events in which the vector boson is produced in association with heavy flavor jets, only events
where the 2 selected central jets are labelled as bb, bc, bl, cc are retained, and defined as V +hf. Therefore,
the phase space of interest for this study is different from what shown in sections 5.1–5.2, where only
b-jets were considered in the final state. The missing transverse energy is reconstructed by taking the
transverse momentum of the negative sum of the four-momenta of all the visible particles, and the missing
transverse momentum is defined consistently summing over all the charged particles.
Three event selections are implemented, corresponding to the relevant three search channels: the 0, 1 and
2-lepton channel selections, corresponding to the Z → νν, the W → `ν, and the Z → `` channels. All
selections require at least 2 central jets, and for the Z → νν and W → `ν ones a veto on a higher number
of jets (central + forward) than 3 is also applied. Further details relative to the specific channels are listed
in the following, where plots of the relevant variables studying the effect of scale variations on Sherpa
2.2, varying µF and µR of a factor of two up or down in different combinations, and comparing the result
with MG5_aMC+Py8 CKKW-L B (for the Z → `` and W → `ν channels) or MG5_aMC+Py8 CKKW-L
A (for the Z → νν channel) are shown. Only shape effects are taken into account for this study, therefore
the distributions are all normalised to the same area.

6 Electroweak Vjj production

The V + jets final state can also be constructed with two additional electroweak couplings at tree level,
giving rise to a gauge-invariant set of Vjj Feynman diagrams, in which the two jets recoil against a t-
channel gauge boson at tree level. This includes the vector boson fusion diagram. Vjj predictions have
been generated using Sherpa 2.2 with up to two extra emissions at LO accuracy and are shown here
together with an estimate of the scale and PDF uncertainties to illustrate the typical size of the generator
uncertainties in regions of phase space probed by ATLAS electroweak Vjj analyses. The samples only
have LO accuracy and do not include any higher-order QCD or electroweak corrections.

6.2 Electroweak Zjj production

Comparisons of 13 TeV predictions for electroweak Zjj production are shown for two regions of phase
space, that have been measured by ATLAS at 8 TeV [47]. The Zjj inclusive region requires at least two
jets with pT > 55 GeV and 45 GeV, respectively. Jets are reconstructed using the anti-kt algorithm using a
jet-radius parameter of 0.4. Furthermore, a Z-boson candidate is constructed from exactly two opposite-
sign same-flavour dressed leptons with pT > 25 GeV and |η| < 2.47 and the invariant mass of the dilepton
system is required to fall within ±10 GeV of the resonance value.
Figure 26 shows the predicted differential cross sections as a function of the jet multiplicity and the
leading jet transverse momentum in the inclusive phase space. Scale and PDF uncertainties have been
estimated in the same way as described in Section 6.1 and are indicated by the orange band.
The Zjj search region is made up from the subset of events that pass the inclusive region as well as a veto
on additional jet-activity in between the two leading jets in order to suppress regular Z+ jets production.
A veto scale of 20 GeV is used for additional jets beyond the two leading jets. The transverse momentum
of the dilepton system is required to be greater than 20 GeV and the vector pT sum of the two leptons and
the two jets, which is normalised by the scalar pT sum, is required to be less than 0.15. Finally, the dijet
invariant mass, m j j , is required to be greater than 1 TeV.
Figure 27 shows the predicted differential cross sections as a function of the dijet invariant mass and the
dijet rapidity difference. The predicted differential cross section as a function of the dilepton pT spectrum
is shown in Figure 28. The uncertainties are at the level of 10–20 % in the inclusive Zjj regions, but
can rise beyond 30 % in the high-mass search region, which has a 1 TeV cut on the dijet invariant mass
and a veto on additional jet activity in the rapidity interval between the two jets in order to suppress
backgrounds, including regular Drell–Yan production.

7 Tuning Pythia8 parameters in matched setups with MG5_aMC+Py8
  CKKW-L

7.1 Motivation

The Pythia8 A14 tune used in ATLAS had the parameter SpaceShower:rapidityOrder turned on. This
forces the initial state radiations (ISR), after the first, to be ordered in rapidity. However, when showering
events generated by MG5_aMC+Py8 CKKW-L, this setting results in non-negligible dependence on the
merging scale [48]. So it was decided to start from the A14 tune, and after turning this particular setting
off, re-tune Pythia8 parameters in Z-boson+jets events. The A14 tune was performed for four different
PDFs, but in ATLAS the version with NNPDF2.3LO is used, and in this note, A14 refers to this particular
variant only.


7.2 Strategies

   1. In the first approach, referred to as Tune (RO), in MG5_aMC+Py8 CKKW-L, Z-boson events
      with up to 3 extra jets were generated, and showered with Pythia8 A14 tune. Then the tuning
      was performed on the complete matched setup, i.e. on the output of Pythia8 interfaced with
      MG5_aMC+Py8 CKKW-L.
   2. In the second approach, referred to as PS-only tune (RO), it was decided to initially decouple the
      two generators. The tuning of Pythia8 was done separately using only Pythia8 output. Then the ob-
      tained tunes were used to shower Z-boson events with up to 3 extra jets generated in MG5_aMC+Py8
      CKKW-L, and the consistency with initial Pythia-eight only tune predictions were checked.

In both cases the Rivet analysis toolkit and the Professor MC tuning system have been used, at ver-
sions 2.5.3 and 2.1.4 respectively. The Pythia version used was 8.219, with the PDFs taken from
LHAPDF version 6.1.3. The MG5_aMC+Py8 CKKW-L parameters used were: Merging:Dparameter=0.2,
Merging:unorderedASscalePrescrip=0, and Merging:TMS=30.

In ATLAS, tunes are performed using the Professor automated tuning tool, where each bin of each
observable is parametrised as a N-dimensional 3rd order polynomial based on the sampled tune points in
the parameter hypercube (N being the number of tuned parameters). These parametrisations are then used
to calculate a χ2 with respect to the reference data, which was numerically minimised in the parameter
space to find the best tune point. Weight factors are used in the χ2 and degree of freedom, Ndf , calculation
to place increased emphasis on certain observables.
The main focus in each case was to describe the distributions sensitive to underlying event in Z-boson
events [49], so most weight was put on that. However, other analyses like measurement of event-shape in
Z-boson events [50] was also looked at, and care was taken so that the Z-boson pT distribution [51] was
                                                                     √
modelled well in each case. These three analyses were performed at s = 7 TeV.
In the first approach, the αS corresponding to ISR, FSR and MPI were set to 0.125, motivated by [52].
Then the multiple parton interaction (MPI) parameter corresponding to pT cutoff,
MultipartonInteractions:pT0Ref and the colour reconnection strength parameter ColourReconnection:range
were tuned.




                                                     33
In the second approach, more Pythia8 parameters were tuned. They divide roughly into the initial and
final state parton shower scales and couplings (SpaceShower:* and TimeShower:* parameters), and multiple
parton interactions (MultipartonInteractions:* parameters). Two “beam-remnant” parameters are also
tuned: the primordialKThard which generates a narrow Gaussian intrinsic transverse momentum for the
hard process incoming partons, and the previously mentioned ColourReconnection:range.
Traditionally, the distribution of mean pT against charged particle multiplicity is found to be very sens-
itive to the modelling of the colour reconnection (CR) in underlying event analyses. The Pythia8 model
for CR has recently undergone a large expansion in [53], and it was decided to test the effect of these
new models as well while tuning. While all the current tunes uses the so called “MPI-based” scheme
(will be referred to as CR0 model subsequently, where the ColourReconnection:range is the tunable para-
meter), two new models of CRs have been introduced, with corresponding tunable parameters. In the
first new one, which adds more degrees of freedom to the MPI-based scheme (will be referred to as CR1
model subsequently), two parameters, ColourReconnection:m0 corresponding to the string length and
ColourReconnection:junctionCorrection are tuned. It was used with ColourReconnection:allowDoubleJunRem
= off setting, as recommended by the authors. In the second new model, termed the “gluon-move scheme"
(will be referred to as CR2 model subsequently), three parameters were tuned: ColourReconnection:m2Lambda,
ColourReconnection:fracGluon, and ColourReconnection:dLambdaCut.

A detailed description of the tuned parameters is beyond the scope of this note, and the reader is referred
to the Pythia8 online documentation.


7.3 Results and discussions

The table 3 documents the result for each of the tuning setups mentioned earlier. In each case, the
parameters not mentioned were fixed at A14 value.
In Fig. 29, the performance of these tunes is shown against different ATLAS measurements. In general,
the best possible tune obtained for each setup give very similar results. The plots of UE in Z-boson events
are most sensitive to MPI. The charged particle sum pT and multiplicity density as a function of Z-boson
pT in toward region show similar trends. Just switching ISR rapidity ordering off clearly deteriorates the
agreement with data considerably, necessitating this tuning effort. For mean pT against charged particle
multiplicity, none of the models predict the right shape, in spite of they having different CR models. This
is in strong contrast to what happens in minimum-bias events [54]. For the thrust distribution at low Z-
boson pT , where it should be most sensitive to MPI, the trend is somewhat reversed, with new tunes doing
a bit worse than the A14 with ISR rapidity ordering off. Most of the tunes, except PS-only tune (RO, CR2)
do very well for Z-boson pT , which is affected mostly by ISR and BBR intrinsic kT . Finally, for inclusive
jet multiplicity in Z-boson events most tune predictions are very similar, but they all underestimate the
data.
In Fig. 30, the effect of varying the merging scale by a factor of two and half on the UE observables in
Z-boson events for PS-only tune (RO, CR0) tune is shown. This confirms little or no dependence on the
merging scale even for the tunes where only Pythia8 output is tuned.
The following points discuss the interesting features and open issues:

   • The robustness of the tunes can be clearly be seen, as tunes obtained by two different approaches
     give very similar predictions.

8 Conclusion

The baseline Monte Carlo simulation setup used by ATLAS to model single electroweak boson production
in association with jets in proton–proton collisions at a centre-of-mass energy of 13 TeV in Run 2 has been
described. Several Monte Carlo generator predictions are compared in regions of phase space relevant
for Run 2 data analyses, but also compared to recent measurements using Run-1 or early Run-2 data that
has been corrected for detector effects. Comparisons are made for regions of phase space with or without
additional requirements on the heavy-flavour content in the accompanying jets, as well as electroweak
V j j production. Large discrepancies between different predictions are highlighted for processes involving
heavy-flavour jets and PDF and scale uncertainties are studied for all processes. In addition, dedicated
sets of tuned Pythia8 parameters in matched setups with MG5_aMC+Py8 CKKW-L are presented and
discussed.
