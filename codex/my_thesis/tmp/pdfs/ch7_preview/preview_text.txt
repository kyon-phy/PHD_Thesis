Multijet background study199
The multijet suppression used in Section 7.1.2 is studied with simulated dijet and leptoquark200
events. The purpose is to identify a selection that removes events with instrumental Emiss
T201
while retaining the signal with neutrinos. The multijet production cross section is large, so202
a small probability for a jet to be misidentified as a τ, combined with a large jet-energy203
mismeasurement, can produce events with the selected final state.204
B.1 Origin of the discriminating variables205
Jet-momentum mismeasurement produces a characteristic direction for the apparent missing206
momentum. For example, if the transverse momentum of one jet is underestimated by δ⃗pj
T, the207
reconstructed momentum imbalance receives a contribution in the direction of the missing part208
of that jet momentum. The resulting ⃗pmiss
T is therefore approximately aligned with the jet. In a209
signal event, the neutrinos produce real missing momentum, whose direction is not fixed by a210
jet-energy mismeasurement.211
The variables ∆φmin and Rmiss/j defined in Eq. 7.3 test these two features. The first measures212
the angular alignment, and the second compares the missing momentum with the momentum213
of the closest jet in azimuth. The combination is useful because a low-pT jet can lie close to214
the direction of real missing momentum in a signal event. Such an event can have small ∆φmin215
but largeR miss/j .216

The residual multijet contribution is examined after applying the cleaning cut and the full SR244
