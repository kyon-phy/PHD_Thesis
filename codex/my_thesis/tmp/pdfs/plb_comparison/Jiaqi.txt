 Section 5

 Why is there no muon veto for hadronic tau candidates?
 → the probability of a muon to be mis-identified as a had tau candidate is much smaller than
 that for an electron. Therefore we only apply eVeto RNN for tau candidates.

 → Will define a calorimeter-tagged muon:

 “calorimeter-tagged muons (identified from ID tracks matched with calorimeter energy

 Why is for an electron only the closest jet vetoes, while for muons all jets close by are vetoed
 if they have max. 2 tracks?
 →Not all jets near a muon are vetoed. We only reject a jet to which a muon is
 ghost-associated to within DeltaR=0.4 and it has not many associated tracks. Electrons
 typically produce sizable calorimeter showers, while muons are usually minimum-ionising.
 This leads to the different strategy for jet-electron and jet-muon overlap removal. We agree
 that the current wording is a bit misleading and propose to revise it to:

 “jets within $De\ta R=0.4$ of a muon if they have at most two associated tracks and the
 muon is ghost-associated with the jet.”

 Event selection

 In general it could use some more motivation for some of the cuts. Now they are mostly
 listed. It is nice to connect the requirements to the background which is being rejected (e.g.
 why max. 4 jets). What kind of criterion was used? I see that the stat. unc. on bkg is kept
 small but assume there was also a specific criterion used to extract signal?
 -> The jet multiplicity is required to suppress top quark background. The high-jet pT and the
 invariant mass requirements are used to select events consistent with an on-shell LQ decay,
 whereas the large mT, mET and deltaphi requirements target the high-mass, back-to-back
 taunu topology for the non-resonant LQ process.
 The specific value for each selection were determined through the SR optimisation by
 scanning the selection thresholds and maximising the expected significance, while requiring
 the statistical uncertainty on bkg. remain below 30%.

 For the m_jthad the light jet is a proxy for the b-quark, otherwise it could not give the
 leptoquark mass. What is the purity of this selection? Have you checked that it would not be
 better to use the highest GN-2 value instead? Maybe that would have more purity?

 → The light jet in SR0b is not selected simply as a proxy for b-quark. For large beta23, the
 SR0b-Res has a sizeable contribution from resonant U1->stau decay. In particular,
 U1->charged lepton decay width for btau:stau:dtau is approximately 1:|beta23|^2:|beta13|^2,
 which means that fraction of stau decay increase from ~3.8% to ~74% when beta23
 increase from 0.2 to 1.8. Selecting the light jets with highest GN-2 value is therefore not
 appropriate for the SR0b.

 Systematic uncertainties

 30% uncertainty on W+HF vs. W+LF. Was a cross-check made for this analysis too? Why
 would these old ATLAS studies be valid for this analysis, in another phase space. It would be
 good to justify this.
 → As shown in Refs. 87-90, mismodeling of W+HF/LF ratio depends on the event
 kinematics indeed. The 30% uncertainty is chosen conservatively based on the largest
 deviations observed in the previous ATLAS studies.
 Since the analysis is stat-dominant, further increasing this uncertainty would have a
 negligible impact on the final result. We also decorrelated this uncertainty between the
 resonant and non-resonant regions to account for possible kinematic dependence.

 For the Z->nunu background, why was the choice made not to use a fake tau method, like
 almost all similar searches do? We know that the modeling is bad and it is confirmed by the
 60% differences you are already seeing. Would it not be better to correct using such a
 method? Now we apply a 100% uncertainty on a MC method. The 100% is also not really
 motivated.
 → The contribution from the fake background in this analysis is negligibly small. Even the
 100% uncertainty would have less than 1% impact on the final result. So we decided not to
 use a dedicated fake tau method for this background. The 100% uncertainty is assigned
 conservatively from the fake study.

 Results

 Motivate why you completely uncorrelated the W normalization (same for top) in the two
 regions. Connected to this. For top the two factors are roughly similar, but for the W they are
 very different. Do you understand why the W normalization in both regions would be so
 different?
 → We decorrelated the normalization in the Res/Non-Res region since they probe different
 kinematics regimes. The difference between W+jets normalisation factors can be explained
 as a result of the different jet-pT range in the two regions.

 →All resonant and non-resonant signals are included simultaneously in the matrix-element

 → The missing ET trigger is already fully efficient for the signal-region selection, since all

 page 5 third paragraph: The contribution from interference between the signal process and

 interference term (I), which scale with mu and sqrt(mu) in the fit, respectively. And the signal
 model is therefore treated as (mu * S + sqrt(mu) * I + B) in the likelihood.
