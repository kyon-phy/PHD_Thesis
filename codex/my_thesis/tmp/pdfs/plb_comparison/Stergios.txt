 Section 5

 Why is there no muon veto for hadronic tau candidates?

 The probability for a muon to be misidentified as a $\tau_{\mathrm{had}}$ candidate is much
 smaller than for an electron. Therefore, only the electron-veto RNN is applied to the
 $\tau_{\mathrm{had}}$ candidates.

 Define a calorimeter-tagged muon before

 We have adjusted the sentence adding a definition for a calorimeter-tagged muon.

 Revised text:  “calorimeter-tagged muons  (identified  from ID tracks matched with a
 calorimeter energy deposit compatible with a minimum ionizing particle)  sharing an ID
 track with an electron”

 Why is for an electron only the closest jet vetoes, while for muons all jets close by are vetoed
 if they have max. 2 tracks?

 Not all jets close to a muon are removed. A jet is removed only if the muon is
 ghost-associated with it within $\Delta R=0.4$ and the jet has at most two associated tracks.

 Event selection

 why max. 4 jets). What kind of criterion was used? I see that the stat. unc. on bkg is kept
 small but assume there was also a specific criterion used to extract signal?

 The upper bounds on the jet multiplicity suppress the top quark background. The high
 jet-$p_{\mathrm{T}}$ and the invariant mass requirements are used to select events
 consistent with an on-shell LQ decay (resonant LQ production), whereas the large
 $m_{\mathrm{T}}$, $E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}$ and $\Delta\phi$ requirements target
 the high-mass, back-to-back taunu topology for the non-resonant LQ process.
 The specific value for each selection was determined through the SR optimisation by
 scanning the selection thresholds and maximising the expected significance, while requiring
 the statistical uncertainty on the total background to remain below 30\%.

 Systematic uncertainties

===== PAGE 5 =====
 30% uncertainty on W+HF vs. W+LF. Was a cross-check made for this analysis too? Why
 would these old ATLAS studies be valid for this analysis, in another phase space. It would be
 good to justify this.

 As shown in Refs. 87-90, indeed the W+HF/W+LF mismodeling depends on the event
 kinematics. The 30\% uncertainty is chosen conservatively to cover the largest deviations
 observed in the previous ATLAS studies.
 Since the analysis is statistically dominant, further increasing this uncertainty has a
 negligible impact on the final result. This uncertainty is also decorrelated between the
 resonant and non-resonant regions to account for possible kinematic dependence.

 For the Z->nunu background, why was the choice made not to use a fake tau method, like
 almost all similar searches do? We know that the modeling is bad and it is confirmed by the
 60% differences you are already seeing. Would it not be better to correct using such a
 method? Now we apply a 100% uncertainty on a MC method. The 100% is also not really
 motivated.

 The contribution from the fake-$\tau$ background in this analysis is negligibly small. Even a
 100\% uncertainty changes the final result by less than 1\%. For this reason, a dedicated
 fake-$\tau$ method is not needed for this background. The 100\% uncertainty is assigned
 conservatively and should cover the differences up to 60\% observed in the fake study.

 regions. Connected to this. For top the two factors are roughly similar, but for the W they are
 very different. Do you understand why the W normalization in both regions would be so

 The normalizations are decorrelated between the Res and Non-Res regions because they
 probe different kinematic regimes. The difference between the fitted W+jets normalization
 factors can be explained as a result of the different jet-$p_{\mathrm{T}}$ and
 $E_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}$ ranges in the two regions.

 It is mentioned that it is verified that the total yield stays positive in all cases, but is this also

 In general, the contribution from BSM+interference could become negative in cases where
 mu is small enough. However, the contribution from interference is small in the parameter
 region to which the analysis is sensitive, and the upper-limit determination is not affected.

 First of all, congratulations on this interesting result by ATLAS. The overall quality of the

 page 2 last paragraph: Two types…
 In the resonant and non-resonant categories, do you consider only the signal that each
 category is dedicated to?
 Similarly, for the b-tagging categories. What is considered as signal in each category and
 what do you do for the other signals? Is each signal extracted from the dedicated category?

 We do not separate the signal according to the analysis categories. The resonant and
 non-resonant SRs are optimised for the corresponding topologies, while both can contain
 contributions from the other topology (although usually much smaller). The same applies to
 the b-tagged and b-vetoed categories. All four SRs are used simultaneously in the signal
 extraction.

 page 4 4rth paragraph:
 How reasonable is it to look only in the left handed scenario?

 The pure left-handed scenario is chosen as the main benchmark since it directly probes the
 $b \rightarrow c \tau \nu$ transition motivating this search. We have also studied the

===== PAGE 7 =====
 alternative benchmark with $\left| \beta_R^{33} \right|=1$, as shown in the supplementary
 material.
 We find that the right-handed coupling enhances the relative non-resonant contribution,
 leading to imporved sensitivity at high LQ masses. We therefore consider the supplementary
 study sufficient to illustrate the impact of this alternative benchmark, while keeping the main
 interpretation focused on the purely left-handed scenario.

 The upper limit on the combined resonant and non-resonant visible cross section therefore

 page 4 last paragraph/ page 5 first: The relative contributions of…
 Can you explain more the interference between resonant and non resonant signal and how
 this is taken into account in the signal extraction.

 All resonant and non-resonant signal diagrams are included simultaneously in the
 matrix-element calculation. Therefore, any interference between resonant and non-resonant
 diagrams is naturally included in the nominal BSM samples, and no specific treatment is
 needed in the signal extraction.

 Therefore, no dedicated hadronic tau trigger is needed for the signal efficiency. Single-lepton
 triggers are used for the CRs and VRs.

 page 5 third paragraph: The contribution from interference between the signal process and
 the SM …

 The interference occurs between the BSM and SM $\tau\nu$ amplitudes.
 Separate samples are used for the pure BSM term (S) and the interference term (I), which
 scale with $\mu$ and $\sqrt(\mu)$ in the fit, respectively. The expected yield is therefore
 written as ($\mu * S + \sqrt(\mu) * I + B$) in the likelihood.
