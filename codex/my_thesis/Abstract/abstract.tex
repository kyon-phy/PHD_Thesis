\begin{abstract}

Measurements of semileptonic $B$-meson decays show tensions with Standard Model predictions for the ratios of decay rates involving $\tau$ leptons to those involving electrons or muons.
These $B$ anomalies motivate leptoquarks, hypothetical bosons that couple quarks to leptons and can explain the anomalies through their contribution to $b\to c\tau\nu$ transitions.
Such particles also arise in grand unified theories and models of quark--lepton unification.

A search for leptoquarks is performed in final states with a hadronically decaying $\tau$ lepton and missing transverse momentum, using $140~\ifb$ of $pp$ collision data collected by ATLAS at the LHC during Run~2 (2015--2018) at $\sqrt{s}=13~\TeV$.
Four signal regions are defined to probe resonant leptoquark production and non-resonant $t$-channel exchange, covering a wide range of leptoquark masses and coupling scenarios.
The backgrounds are estimated using simulation, with the normalisations of the dominant backgrounds constrained by data.

No significant excess above the Standard Model predictions is observed in any of the four signal regions.
Observed upper limits at 95\% confidence level are set on the couplings of the benchmark $U_1$ vector-leptoquark model for masses between $1.5$ and $3.0~\TeV$.
This search also provides the first ATLAS limits in the $(\beta_L^{23},\gU)$ plane from the $\tau\nu(+b)$ final state, extending the constraints into parameter regions not covered by previous leptoquark searches.

\end{abstract}
