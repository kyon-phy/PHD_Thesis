\chapter{Signal--SM interference}
\label{app:signal_interference_study}

The interference between the leptoquark and SM amplitudes changes the signal contribution remaining after the selection.
Its effect on the $\bTagSR$ optimisation in Section~\ref{sec:ch7_sr1b} is examined here, together with the role of the kinematic requirements in reducing this contribution.

\section{Contribution to the selected signal yield}

The selected signal prediction includes the interference term in Eq.~\ref{eq:signal_interference}.
If $S$ denotes the pure BSM yield and $I$ the interference yield, the excess above the SM prediction is $S+I$ at the nominal signal normalisation.
For destructive interference, $I<0$, so a selection ranked using $S$ alone can overestimate the final sensitivity.
The interference contribution is therefore checked after the optimisation and included in the final statistical model.
The signal efficiencies in Chapter~\ref{chapter:event_selection} are evaluated using the pure BSM contribution because the signed interference yield is not an event-selection efficiency.

\section{Effect of the kinematic requirements}

The low-energy phase space in which interference with SM $W$ production is most important is suppressed by the requirements on $\pT^\tau$, $\met$ and $\mT$.
In particular, the non-resonant requirements $\pT^\tau>200~\GeV$, $\met>400~\GeV$ and $\mT>600~\GeV$ retain the hard tail while reducing the destructive contribution.

At $\MU=1.5~\TeV$, the magnitude of the interference contribution in $\bTagSR$-$\Res$ is typically below $5\%$ of the pure BSM yield over the parameter points studied.
In $\bTagSR$-$\NonRes$, fractions as large as $40\%$ are found at the lowest $\gU$ values, where the signal yield is too small to be excluded by the analysis.
Near the expected exclusion boundary from $\bTagSR$-$\NonRes$ alone, the contribution is typically below $10\%$ at $\MU=1.5~\TeV$ and below $5\%$ at $\MU=3.0~\TeV$.
The expected limits obtained with and without interference are found to differ little in the coupling range to which the one-$b$-jet analysis is sensitive.
The pure BSM yield is therefore used to rank the trial selections, while interference is retained in the final signal prediction.

This conclusion does not apply to the $\bVetoSR$ regions, where the interference contribution must be retained, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:ch7_sr0b}.
The supplementary $\met$ scan in Appendix~\ref{app:sr0b_optimisation} illustrates how a tighter requirement can reduce this contribution in the zero-$b$-jet regions.
