The SR0b regions select signal events with no b-tagged jet and extend the sensitivity to large129
β 23
L . As shown by the flavour current and coupling texture in Eqs. 3.6 and 3.7, β 23
L controls the130
sτ interaction and contributes Vcsβ 23

were introduced in Section 3.1.5. In particular, U1 →sτ produces a resonant signal with132

events in which a b-jet is not tagged or reconstructed. The leading jet in SR0b is not explicitly134

with a leading c jet. At (MU ,gU ,β 23

s-jet contribution contains the sτ resonance, whereas the c-jet contribution can contain an138
on-shell U1 →cν τ decay. For the latter, the resonance is in the cντ system rather than the jet–τ139
system. In the non-resonant truth-level category, approximately 33% and 11% of the events140
have a radiated leading s or c jet, with most of the remainder containing a gluon jet in sc→τν τ141
production. These fractions describe the chosen benchmark and truth selection, rather than a142
flavour measurement of the reconstructed jets.143

B.3 Residual multijet contribution237
The residual multijet contribution is examined after applying the cleaning cut and the full SR238
requirements. No simulated multijet events survive those selections in the samples used for the239
study. This supports neglecting the multijet contribution in the nominal SR prediction, but zero240
surviving simulated events does not establish an exactly zero physical rate or a quantitative241
upper bound without the effective sample size.242
Jets misidentified asτ candidates can still occur in other background processes. In particular,243
Z→νν production with jets contains genuine missing momentum and can pass the multijet244
cleaning even when the selected τ candidate is a jet. This contribution is included in the245
simulated Z+jets background. A supplementary selection requiring Medium τ identification246
while failing Tight also gives a negligible multijet contribution in the simulated study, supporting247
the effectiveness of the cleaning beyond the nominal Tight selection.248

Appendix D267
Signal–SM interference268
The interference between the leptoquark and SM amplitudes changes the signal contribution269
remaining after the selection. Its effect on the SR1b optimisation in Section 7.2 is examined270
here, together with the role of the kinematic requirements in reducing this contribution.271
D.1 Contribution to the selected signal yield272
The selected signal prediction includes the interference term in Eq. 5.4. If S denotes the pure273
BSM yield and I the interference yield, the excess above the SM prediction is S+I at the274
nominal signal normalisation. For destructive interference, I<0 , so a selection ranked using S275
alone can overestimate the final sensitivity. The interference contribution is therefore checked276
after the optimisation and included in the final statistical model. The signal efficiencies in277
Chapter 7 are evaluated using the pure BSM contribution because the signed interference yield278
is not an event-selection efficiency.279
D.2 Effect of the kinematic requirements280
The low-energy phase space in which interference with SM W production is most important is281
suppressed by the requirements on pτ
T, Emiss
T and mT. In particular, the non-resonant require-282
ments pτ
T >200 GeV , Emiss
T >400 GeV and mT >600 GeV retain the hard tail while reducing283
the destructive contribution.284

below 5% of the pure BSM yield over the parameter points studied. InSR1b-NonRes, fractions286
as large as 40% are found at the lowest gU values, where the signal yield is too small to be287
excluded by the analysis. Near the expected exclusion boundary from SR1b-NonRes alone, the288
