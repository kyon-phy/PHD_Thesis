\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chapter:conclusion}

The search presented in this thesis is the first ATLAS search dedicated to high-mass $U_1$ leptoquarks in the $\tau\nu(+b)$ final state with a hadronically decaying $\tau$ and missing transverse momentum.
It uses $140~\ifb$ of $pp$ collision data recorded during Run~2 (2015--2018) at $\sqrt{s}=13~\TeV$.
The search is motivated by the $\RD$ anomalies, which can be explained by a $U_1$ contribution to $b\to c\tau\nu$.

Both resonant production and non-resonant $t$-channel exchange are probed, extending the sensitivity across different coupling scenarios.
For each topology, two SRs are defined by requiring one $b$-jet or applying a $b$-jet veto, giving four SRs in total to gain better sensitivity.
The optimisation is performed for $\bTagSR$ using a grid search for maximum sensitivity.
The fake-$\hadtau$ background, mainly from $\znunu$+jets, is small. The multijet background is negligible after the full SR selections.
The remaining dominant $\wjets$ and top-quark backgrounds are estimated using NFs measured in the corresponding CRs, and their extrapolation is tested in dedicated VRs.

No significant excess above the SM prediction is observed.
In the $\bVetoSR$-\Res and $\bVetoSR$-\NonRes regions, 33 and 45 events are observed, with expected backgrounds of $36.4\pm7.3$ and $38.3\pm9.9$ events, respectively.
In the $\bTagSR$-\Res and $\bTagSR$-\NonRes regions, 3 and 2 events are observed, compared to background expectations of $2.69\pm0.83$ and $1.88\pm0.71$ events, respectively.
A profile-likelihood fit including statistical and systematic uncertainties is performed to extract the limit on the signal strength $\mu$.
Upper limits are set at 95\% CL on the couplings for $\MU$ between $1.5$ and $3.0~\TeV$ in the pure-left-handed scenario.
The limits are obtained using the CL$_{\mathrm{s}}$ method with pseudo-experiments.
At $\MU=1.5~\TeV$, the observed (expected) upper limits on $\gU$ are approximately $2.8\ (2.8)$ for $\beta_L^{23}=0.2$, $1.13\ (1.11)$ for $\beta_L^{23}=1.0$ and $0.52\ (0.53)$ for $\beta_L^{23}=2.2$.
At $\MU=2.5~\TeV$, the corresponding limits are approximately $4.0\ (3.9)$ for $\beta_L^{23}=0.2$, $2.30\ (2.19)$ for $\beta_L^{23}=1.0$ and $1.22\ (1.18)$ for $\beta_L^{23}=2.2$.

An interpretation in the right-handed scenario in $\bTagSR$ is also performed using the asymptotic approximation, excluding the interference effect.
At $\MU=1.5~\TeV$, the observed (expected) upper limits on $\gU$ are $2.20\ (2.18)$, $0.84\ (0.80)$ and $0.53\ (0.51)$ for $\beta_L^{23}=0.2$, $1.0$ and $2.2$, respectively.
At $\MU=3.0~\TeV$, the corresponding limits are approximately $4.3\ (4.2)$ for $\beta_L^{23}=0.2$, $1.98\ (1.96)$ for $\beta_L^{23}=1.0$ and $1.30\ (1.28)$ for $\beta_L^{23}=2.2$.


These results probe large values of $\beta_L^{23}$ in a region of parameter space not covered by previous $\tau\tau$ searches.
The $\bTagSR$ regions provide most of the sensitivity, while $\bVetoSR$ improves the sensitivity when the $s\tau$ and $c\nu_\tau$ couplings are enhanced at large $\betaL$.
The $\tau\nu(+b)$ channel therefore complements the existing searches.

Further improvements are expected from larger datasets and a dedicated optimisation of $\bVetoSR$. In particular, the gain from the optimisation is expected to be significant in the low-mass region.
