\chapter{Systematic Uncertainties}
\label{chapter:systematics_uncertainties}

Systematic uncertainties in this analysis include the experimental and theoretical uncertainties.
Their sources and evaluation are discussed in this chapter.

\section{Experimental uncertainties}
\label{sec:ch9_experimental}

Experimental uncertainties originate from the detector response and object calibrations. They are propagated through the event weights or reconstructed objects.
The evaluation and treatment of these uncertainties are summarised below.
The values quoted below are the largest changes in the total background yield for each variation direction, taken over individual uncertainty sources and all SRs, CRs and VRs before the fit.

\subsubtitle{Luminosity and pile-up}
\label{subsec:ch9_lumi_pileup}
The 2015-2018 integrated luminosity has an uncertainty of $0.83\%$, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:dataset_selection}.
It is applied to the signal and background normalisation.
The pile-up uncertainty is evaluated by varying the data-derived $\mu$ profile used to calculate the pile-up correction weights for simulated events.

\subsubtitle{Hadronic tau energy scale and efficiencies}
\label{subsec:ch9_tau}
The TES uncertainty in the low-$\pT$ region is measured using $\ztautau$ events, while the uncertainty in the high-$\pT$ region is evaluated using test-beam data and MC comparisons~\cite{Hubner2018TauPerformance,ATL-PHYS-PUB-2015-045}.
In this analysis, the TES uncertainties change the total background yield by approximately $\pm21\%$ for $\pm1\sigma$ variations.
Efficiency uncertainties are evaluated for $\hadtau$ reconstruction and RNN identification using $\ttbar$ events, as well as for electron rejection.
For the \texttt{Tight} ID working point, the RNN identification uncertainties change the total background yield by approximately $\pm6.1\%$ for $\pm1\sigma$ variations.

\subsubtitle{Jets and missing transverse momentum}
\label{subsec:ch9_jets_met}
The JES and JER uncertainties are evaluated using the calibration methods described in Section~\ref{sec:ch6_jets}~\cite{ATLAS2021JetCalibration}.
The JES uncertainties change the total background yield by approximately $\mp4.3\%$ for $\pm1\sigma$ variations.
The JER uncertainties change the total background yield by approximately $\pm9.1\%$ for $\pm1\sigma$ variations.
The $\mpt$ uncertainty is evaluated by recalculating the $\mpt$ for each uncertainty from the hard-object momenta and the soft term.
The soft-term uncertainty is derived from measurements in $\zee$ events~\cite{ATLAS2025METPerformance}.
It changes the total background yield by approximately $\mp1.1\%$ for $\pm1\sigma$ variations.
The effects of hard-object variations on $\mpt$ are included in the corresponding object uncertainties.

\subsubtitle{Jet flavour tagging}
\label{subsec:ch9_ftag}
The flavour-tagging uncertainties are evaluated separately for the $b$-jet tagging efficiency and the $c$-jet and light-jet mistagging efficiencies.
They include calibration sample statistics, background contamination and modelling uncertainties in the efficiency measurements described in Section~\ref{sec:ch6_btag}~\cite{ATLAS2026GN2}.
For the GN2 $85\%$ working point, the $\pm1\sigma$ variations in the $b$-jet tagging, $c$-jet mistagging and light-jet mistagging uncertainties change the total background yield by approximately $\pm2.3\%$, $\pm2.9\%$ and $\pm8.2\%$, respectively.

\subsubtitle{Electrons and muons}
\label{subsec:ch9_leptons}
Electron efficiency uncertainties are evaluated using $\zee$ and $J/\psi\to ee$ events, while energy-scale and resolution uncertainties are obtained by comparing the measured and simulated calorimeter response~\cite{ATLAS2019ElectronPerformance}.
The $\pm1\sigma$ variations in the electron energy-scale and resolution uncertainties change the total background yield by approximately $\pm1.0\%$ and $\mp0.21\%$, respectively.
The muon efficiency, momentum-scale and resolution uncertainties are determined using $\zmumu$ and $J/\psi\to\mu\mu$ events~\cite{ATLAS2021MuonID,ATLAS2023MuonCalibration}.
The $\pm1\sigma$ variations in the muon momentum-scale and resolution uncertainties change the total background yield by approximately $\pm0.15\%$ and $\pm3.7\%$, respectively.
In particular, the resolution uncertainty has a sizeable effect on the high-$\pT$ muons in this analysis.



\section{Theoretical uncertainties}
\label{sec:ch9_theory}

Theoretical uncertainties describe changes in the predicted yields caused by modelling uncertainties in the hard-scattering calculation, parton shower and hadronisation.
Specifically, for the $\wjets$ and top-quark backgrounds, these uncertainties are evaluated through their effects on the extrapolation from the CRs, which is expressed by the transfer factor (TF) as defined in Eq.~(\ref{eq:ch9_transfer}).
\begin{align}
    TF=\frac{N_{bg}^{SR(VR),MC}}{N_{bg}^{CR,MC}},
    \label{eq:ch9_transfer}
\end{align}
where the numerator and denominator are the expected background yields in the SR(VR) and its CR, respectively.

Suppose a theoretical uncertainty changes the background yield by $\Delta N_{bg}=N_{bg}^{sysMC}-N_{bg}^{nomMC}$, where $nomMC$ and $sysMC$ denote the nominal and alternative background predictions, respectively.
For each theoretical variation, the change of TF is calculated as
\begin{align*}
    \frac{TF+\Delta TF}{TF}
    &=
    \frac{N_{bg}^{SR(VR),sysMC}/N_{bg}^{SR(VR),nomMC}}
         {N_{bg}^{CR,sysMC}/N_{bg}^{CR,nomMC}}.
\end{align*}
A variation that changes the background yields by the same fraction in the SR and its corresponding CR gives a double ratio of unity, so that $\Delta TF/TF=0$. The extrapolation uncertainty of such a background variation in the SRs is therefore cancelled.
In case the variation is not cancelled, the remaining uncertainty $\Delta TF/TF$ is evaluated and used as a relative theoretical uncertainty applied only to the background yield in the SR. The values of the relative uncertainties are summarised in Tables~\ref{tab:ch9_theory_res} and~\ref{tab:ch9_theory_nonres} for the resonant and non-resonant regions, respectively.

Note that no theoretical uncertainties are assigned to $\wjets$ and top-quark contributions in the $\CRTop$ and $\CRW$ CRs, which contribute less than $5\%$ and $2\%$ of the total background yield, respectively.


\subsection{$\wjets$}
\label{subsec:ch9_wjets}
The $\wjets$ theoretical uncertainties are evaluated by considering several items.
QCD scale variations refer to variations of the renormalisation and factorisation scales.
PDF uncertainties include variations of the PDF sets and $\alpha_s$ used for the hard process calculation.
Electroweak corrections include the loop contributions from photons and weak bosons. And an additional normalisation uncertainty introduced by requiring one or more heavy-flavour-tagged jets is considered in this analysis.
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{QCD scales:} the uncertainty is evaluated from the envelope of seven-point predictions obtained by varying the matrix-element and parton-shower renormalisation and factorisation scales~\cite{PMGR-2021-01}.
    \item \textbf{PDF:} the uncertainty is evaluated using the Hessian NNPDF3.0 NNLO uncertainty scheme, including alternative PDF sets and two alternative $\alpha_s$ values.
    \item \textbf{Electroweak corrections:} the uncertainty is evaluated from the change in the prediction when approximate NLO electroweak corrections are applied.
    \item \textbf{$W+\mathrm{HF}/W+\mathrm{LF}$ ratio:} an uncertainty is assigned to the heavy-to-light-flavour ratio when extrapolating from the $b$-vetoed $\CRW$ to regions with $b$-jets.
    A value of $30\%$ is adopted, motivated by flavour-dependent normalisation differences observed in an earlier ATLAS measurement~\cite{HIGG-2020-20}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Top-quark background}
\label{subsec:ch9_top}
The top-quark background theoretical uncertainties are evaluated by considering several items.
QCD scale and PDF variations follow the definitions in Section~\ref{subsec:ch9_wjets}.
The NLO matching variation changes the shower veto definition used to avoid overlap between radiation generated by \textsc{Powheg} and \textsc{Pythia}.
Parton shower and hadronisation variations compare different models for radiation and the formation of hadrons.
ISR and final-state radiation variations change the shower $\alpha_s$ value and its renormalisation scale, respectively.
The $h_{\mathrm{damp}}$ variation changes the modelling of the first hard emission.
The $\ttbar$ NNLO variation changes the predicted shape of the top quark $\pT$ distribution.
The shower recoil variation changes which particle absorbs recoil from gluon emissions in top quark decays.
The interference variation replaces the nominal diagram removal (DR) method with diagram subtraction (DS) for the overlapping contributions in the simulations of $\ttbar$ and $Wt$ processes.
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{QCD scales, PDF and PDF $\alpha_s$:} the uncertainties are evaluated by reweighting the nominal samples, as for $\wjets$.
    \item \textbf{Matrix element (NLO matching):} the uncertainty is evaluated by changing the definition of the region in which \textsc{Pythia} shower emissions are vetoed to avoid overlap with radiation generated by \textsc{Powheg}.
    \item \textbf{Parton shower and hadronisation:} the uncertainty is obtained by comparing the nominal samples with alternative samples using \textsc{Herwig}~7.
    \item \textbf{ISR (FSR) $\alpha_s$:} the uncertainty is evaluated using internal weights for initial-state (final-state) radiation.
    \item \textbf{$h_{\mathrm{damp}}$:} the uncertainty is evaluated by varying $h_{\mathrm{damp}}$, which regulates hard radiation recoiling against the $\ttbar$ system~\cite{ATL-PHYS-PUB-2020-023}.
    \item \textbf{$\ttbar$ NNLO:} the uncertainty is derived by reweighting the nominal sample to the NNLO prediction~\cite{Czakon:2017wor}.
    \item \textbf{$\ttbar$ shower recoil:} the uncertainty is evaluated by assigning the recoil from subsequent gluon emissions to the parent top quark instead of the $b$ quark in the top quark decay.
    \item \textbf{$Wt$--$\ttbar$ interference:} the uncertainty is evaluated by comparing the nominal $Wt$ sample with an alternative sample in which the overlapping $\ttbar$ contribution is subtracted~\cite{Frixione:2008yi}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Other backgrounds and signal}
\label{subsec:ch9_signal}
The $\znunu$ background with a jet misidentified as a $\hadtau$ is assigned a $100\%$ normalisation uncertainty, while diboson and the remaining $\ztautau$ backgrounds are assigned a conservative $30\%$ normalisation uncertainty.
Signal uncertainties are evaluated using QCD scale and PDF variations, resulting in a $10$--$20\%$ uncertainty.
