\chapter{Event Selection}
\label{chapter:event_selection}
This chapter defines the signal regions (SRs) used to select the final states produced by the processes shown in Figure~\ref{fig:u1_taunu_target_topologies}, which contain a $\hadtau$, missing transverse momentum and jets that may include $b$-jets. Four SRs are defined according to the presence of a $b$-jet and the targeted signal topology, either resonant production or non-resonant exchange. They are denoted $\bTagSR$-\Res, $\bTagSR$-\NonRes, $\bVetoSR$-\Res and $\bVetoSR$-\NonRes.

The baseline selection is introduced first, followed by the analysis variables, the optimisation of the one-$b$-jet regions ($b$-tagged, $\bTagSR$) and the definition of the zero-$b$-jet regions ($b$-veto, $\bVetoSR$).

\section{Baseline selection and analysis variables}
\label{sec:ch7_baseline_selection}

\subsection{Multijet suppression and baseline selection}
\label{subsec:ch7_baseline_selection}

The SR definitions in this analysis are based on the optimisation of selection thresholds on multiple variables.
And the baseline selection is the common starting point for the SR optimisation. 
The common choices for the baseline selection requirements are based on the signal topology and separation from the SM background. 
Beyond that, requirements that suppress backgrounds containing the misidentified objects are also important since these backgrounds can be difficult to model with simulation. In this analysis, multijet events are one such background. Therefore, dedicated requirements to suppress multijet events need to be applied in the baseline selection.

Specifically, although the high $\met$ threshold required in this analysis rejects most multijet events, some can still enter the SRs due to the large multijet cross-section. These events typically contain a misidentified $\hadtau$ and fake $\met$ due to the poor jet resolution. 
The key point of separating multijet events from signals is therefore the source of fake $\met$.

Fake $\met$ can be produced when the $\pT$ of an energetic jet is underestimated.
In this case, $\mpt$ points approximately towards that jet and its magnitude tends not to be too large compared with the $\pT$ of the mismeasured jet.
On the other hand, in signal events, $\mpt$ from neutrinos has no such tendency.
Figure~\ref{fig:ch7_fake_met} illustrates this difference. The mismeasured part of the energetic jet could mimic the $\met$ from neutrinos. The minimum jet--$\mpt$ separation in $\phi$ and the ratio $\metOverMinDphiJetPt$ are therefore used as discriminating variables. Here $\minDphiJetPt$ is the $\pT$ of the jet with the smallest $\DphiJetMet$.
The $\hadtau$ candidate is also included when evaluating these two variables.

As a result, events are retained if
\begin{equation*}
    \minDphiJetMet\geq0.4 \quad\text{or}\quad \metOverMinDphiJetPt\geq6.
\end{equation*}

The baseline selection therefore requires the event requirements listed below, including the multijet suppression requirement. The final SRs are obtained by applying the additional requirements determined by the optimisation to these baseline events.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item Data events are required to belong to GRLs, which contain the data-taking periods in which the relevant detector systems were operating normally.
  \item Each event is required to contain a reconstructed primary vertex satisfying the definition in Section~\ref{sec:ch6_primary_vertex}.
  \item The lowest-threshold unprescaled $\met$ trigger is used to select events with large $\met$.
  \item $\met>200~\GeV$ to select events in the plateau region of the $\met$ trigger efficiency.
  \item Exactly one baseline $\hadtau$ candidate, which must pass \texttt{Tight} RNN identification.
  \item Baseline electron/muon veto.
  \item Exactly one $b$-jet for the $\bTagSR$.
  \item At least one jet and a $b$-jet veto for the $\bVetoSR$.
  \item Reject events with $\minDphiJetMet<0.4$ and $\metOverMinDphiJetPt<6$ to suppress multijet events.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Analysis variables and distributions}
\label{subsec:ch7_variables}

Multiple variables are used to search for signals and separate them from SM backgrounds.
$\pT^{\hadtau}$ and $\met$ are considered first, since energetic $\tau$ leptons and neutrinos are expected in the signal.
The transverse mass $\mT$ is used to probe the high-energy tail of the $\tau\nu$ final state, which is sensitive to non-resonant leptoquark contributions~\cite{Endo2022NonResonant}.
A tighter $\mT$ threshold is therefore expected in $\bTagSR$-$\NonRes$ than in $\bTagSR$-$\Res$.
The $\mT$ of the $\hadtau$ and $\mpt$ system is defined as
\begin{equation*}
    \mT = \sqrt{2\pT^{\hadtau}\met\left[1-\cos\DphiTauMet\right]},
\end{equation*}
where $\DphiTauMet$ is the separation in $\phi$ between the $\hadtau$ momentum and $\mpt$.

The invariant masses $m_{b\hadtau}$ and $m_{j\hadtau}$ are used to probe resonant $U_1\to b\tau$ and $U_1\to s\tau$ decays in the $b$-tagged and $b$-veto SRs, respectively. They are defined as:
    \begin{equation*}
        m_{b\hadtau} = \sqrt{(\boldsymbol{p}_b+\boldsymbol{p}_{\hadtau})^2}, \qquad m_{j\hadtau} = \sqrt{(\boldsymbol{p}_{j_1}+\boldsymbol{p}_{\hadtau})^2},
    \end{equation*}
where $\boldsymbol{p}_b$ and $\boldsymbol{p}_{\hadtau}$ are the four-momenta of the $b$-jet and $\hadtau$, respectively, and $\boldsymbol{p}_{j_1}$ is the four-momentum of the leading jet.

The $b$-jet $\pT$ is also used. An energetic $b$-jet from the leptoquark decay is expected in the resonant process, whereas the $b$-jet is typically softer in the non-resonant process. 

An upper limit on the jet multiplicity is applied to further suppress the background, especially backgrounds containing a second hadronically decaying $\tau$ lepton reconstructed as a jet, such as $\ttbar$ or $Wt$.

$\DphiTauMet$ is used to select the back-to-back $\hadtau$ and $\mpt$ configuration of the non-resonant signal.

The signal has higher $\pT^{\hadtau}$ and $\mT$ than the background, and favours a back-to-back $\hadtau$ and $\mpt$ configuration.
The signal $m_{b\hadtau}$ distribution is broad because of variations in the momentum carried away by the neutrino from the $\tau$ decay and poor jet reconstruction resolution.

\section[$\bTagSR$ optimisation and definition]{$\bTagSR$ optimisation and definition}
\label{sec:ch7_sr1b}

Event selection in $\bTagSR$ is optimised using a grid scan of thresholds on variables introduced above. $\bTagSR$-$\Res$ and $\bTagSR$-$\NonRes$ are optimised separately.

\subsection{Signal benchmarks and optimisation procedure}
\label{subsec:ch7_scan}

Different signal benchmarks are used for the optimisations of each signal region. The optimisation for $\bTagSR$-$\Res$ uses $(\MU,\gU,\beta_L^{23})=(1.5~\TeV,1.5,0.6)$, where on-shell production is dominant.
For $\bTagSR$-$\NonRes$, the optimisation uses $(\MU,\gU,\beta_L^{23})=(2.5~\TeV,2.5,1.0)$, where $t$-channel exchange is dominant.
The interference is not included in the scan because its contribution is around $5$--$10\%$ of the BSM signals in the parameter range probed by the $\bTagSR$ regions.
% As discussed in Appendix~\ref{app:signal_interference_study}. 
The selected cut combination is therefore not expected to change significantly when the interference is included.
A finer mass threshold scan is also not expected to improve the sensitivity significantly due to the broad mass distribution as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:ch7_scan_variables}(\subref{fig:ch7_scan_variables_mbtau}). The $m_{b\hadtau}$ threshold in $\bTagSR$-$\Res$ is therefore fixed at $800~\GeV$.
The thresholds used for the grid scan are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:ch7_scan_variables}.

The expected sensitivity of each cut combination is evaluated using the significance $Z$ for an integrated luminosity of $140~\ifb$, calculated as follows~\cite{Basso2022Significance}:
\begin{equation}
    Z = \sqrt{2\left[n\ln\!\left(\frac{n(b+\sigma^2)}{b^2+n\sigma^2}\right)-\frac{b^2}{\sigma^2}\ln\!\left(\frac{b^2+n\sigma^2}{b(b+\sigma^2)}\right)\right]},
    \label{eq:ch7_optimisation_significance}
\end{equation}
where $s$ and $b$ are the expected signal and background event yields, $n=s+b$, and the background uncertainty is assumed to be $\sigma=0.3b$.

Although tighter thresholds can give a higher significance in some cases, the corresponding background yields also become very small.
The results are then more strongly affected by the limited MC statistics, which makes the predictions unreliable.
The background yield is therefore required to be greater than 2 events with an integrated luminosity of $140~\ifb$ during the scan. And the relative MC statistical uncertainty on the total background yield remains below $30\%$ for the final results.

Examples of the significance as a function of the threshold are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:ch7_discriminants_with_significance}, along with the $N-1$ $\met$ and $\mT$ distributions in $\bTagSR$-$\Res$ and $\bTagSR$-$\NonRes$, respectively.\footnote{For a selection with $N$ requirements, an $N-1$ distribution is obtained by removing the requirement on the plotted variable while keeping the other $N-1$ requirements. Here, the baseline selection is retained, and any additional $\met$ or $\mT$ requirement is removed.}
For each point in the lower significance panels, $Z$ is calculated using the signal and background yields summed above the corresponding threshold. Points with $b\leq2$ are excluded from the cut choice.
Among the points with $b>2$, the largest $Z$ is obtained at $\met>200~\GeV$ for $\bTagSR$-$\Res$ and $\mT>600~\GeV$ for $\bTagSR$-$\NonRes$.

\section[b-veto signal region (SR0b)]{$b$-veto signal region ($\bVetoSR$)}
\label{sec:ch7_sr0b}

The $\bVetoSR$ regions extend the search to signal events without a selected $b$-jet.
The definitions of $\bVetoSR$ are derived from the optimised $\bTagSR$ with the requirement of one $b$-jet replaced by a $b$-jet veto.
In $\bVetoSR$-$\Res$, the $b$-jet is replaced by the leading jet in the $\pT$ and invariant-mass requirements, giving $\pT^{j_1}>250~\GeV$ and $m_{j\hadtau}>800~\GeV$.
In $\bVetoSR$-$\NonRes$, a leading jet with $50<\pT^{\mathrm{jet}}<250~\GeV$ is selected.
No additional optimisation is applied to the $\bVetoSR$ selections.
The final selections for all SRs are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:ch7_signal_regions}.



Interference with the SM is larger in $\bVetoSR$ than in $\bTagSR$ and is evaluated at each signal parameter point.
Its contribution reduces the pure-BSM signal yield by approximately $20\%$ at $\MU=1.5~\TeV$ and by about $30$--$40\%$ at higher masses.
Therefore, the sum of the pure-BSM and interference contributions remains positive over the parameter region probed by this analysis.

Regarding the background composition, the $\bVetoSR$ is dominated by $W\to\tau\nu$+jets, since vetoing $b$-jets removes much of the top-quark background while retaining $W$ production with light-flavour jets.
Before the fit, $W$+jets contributes approximately $72\%$ in $\bVetoSR$-$\Res$ and $84\%$ in $\bVetoSR$-$\NonRes$.
In the resonant region, diboson production contributes about $12\%$, $Z\to\nu\nu$+jets about $9\%$, and $Z\to\tau\tau$+jets about $6\%$.
In the non-resonant region, the corresponding contributions are approximately $3\%$, $5\%$ and $7\%$.
The $Z\to\nu\nu$, $Z\to\tau\tau$ and diboson events have a similar topology to that in $\bTagSR$, while the top-quark background contribution is negligible.

The flavour of the leading jet is examined to understand the signal contributions in $\bVetoSR$.
The parton-ID assigned to the jet is used, where 3, 4 and 21 denote $s(\bar{s})$ quarks, $c(\bar{c})$ quarks and gluons, respectively.
The distributions for the signal at $(\MU,\gU,\beta_L^{23})=(1.5~\TeV,1.5,0.6)$ are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:ch7_sr0b_truth_jet_flavour}.

In $\bVetoSR$-$\Res$, approximately $30\%$ of the events contain a leading $s$-jet and $60\%$ contain a leading $c$-jet. The $s$-jet contribution is produced through single leptoquark production followed by $U_1\to s\tau$ decays, which is enhanced by large $\beta_L^{23}$ and $g_U$ ($\beta_L^{33}$ is fixed to $1.0$).
For the $c$-jet contribution, a high-$\pT$ $c$-jet is produced in an on-shell $U_1\to c\bar{\nu}_\tau$ decay. Figure~\ref{fig:u1_taunu_target_topologies}(\subref{fig:u1_taunu_resonant_cnu}) shows the diagram of the $bg\to c\tau\nu$ production, which is also enhanced by large $\beta_L^{23}$ and $g_U$.
The $\bVetoSR$-$\Res$ therefore becomes more important as $\beta_L^{23}$ increases.

In $\bVetoSR$-$\NonRes$, approximately $33\%$ of the events are produced through non-resonant $\tau\nu$ production with a $c$-jet from initial-state radiation (ISR), while $11\%$ contain an ISR $s$-jet.
The remaining events are mainly produced through $sc\to\tau\nu_\tau$ with an ISR gluon jet.
