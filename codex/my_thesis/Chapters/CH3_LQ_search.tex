\chapter{Leptoquark Search at the LHC}
\label{chapter:LQ_search}

\section{Leptoquark Models and Phenomenology}
\label{sec:leptoquarks}

\subsection{General properties of leptoquarks}
\label{subsec:lq_general}

Leptoquarks~(LQs), introduced in Section~\ref{subsec:leptoquarks_motivation}, are bosons that couple a quark directly to a lepton and therefore mediate interactions connecting the quark and lepton sectors.
Depending on their spin, leptoquarks can be classified as scalar leptoquarks with spin zero or vector leptoquarks with spin one. 
They appear in several theoretical frameworks, including grand unified theories such as $SU(5)$ and $SO(10)$, quark--lepton unification models such as the Pati--Salam model, composite models, and models motivated by flavour anomalies~\cite{PatiSalam1974,GeorgiGlashow1974SU5,Dorsner2016}.


Leptoquarks are particularly interesting in the context of semileptonic flavour physics. 
Their tree-level exchange can generate effective four-fermion interactions involving two quark fields and two lepton fields. 
Such interactions can modify flavour observables in processes such as $b\rightarrow c\tau\nu$ and $b\rightarrow s\ell^{+}\ell^{-}$. 
The flavour structure of the LQ couplings is model dependent. 
As noted in Section~\ref{subsec:leptoquarks_motivation}, precision flavour measurements strongly constrain the couplings to the first two generations, so LQ models motivated by the $b\rightarrow c\tau\nu$ anomalies favour interactions with the top quark, bottom quark and $\tau$ lepton~\cite{Cornella2019}.

Since they carry colour charge, LQs can be pair-produced through QCD interactions, and their couplings to quarks and leptons allow single production and non-resonant exchange. 
Collider searches therefore complement low-energy flavour measurements. 
Flavour observables probe virtual LQ effects at low momentum transfer, while $pp$ collisions probe direct LQ production and high-energy virtual exchange.

\subsection{The simplified $U_{1}$ leptoquark model}
\label{subsec:u1_simplified_model}

Several leptoquark representations have been studied in connection with semileptonic $B$-meson anomalies, including the scalar singlet $S_{1}$ and the scalar doublet $R_{2}$~\cite{Sakaki2013LQ}. 
In this thesis, however, the benchmark interpretation is based on the vector singlet leptoquark $U_{1}$. 
The $U_{1}$ benchmark is chosen because its left-handed coupling to quark and lepton doublets generates the SM-like vector operator for $b\rightarrow c\tau\nu$. 
Its enhanced couplings to third-generation fermions also lead to final states with $\tau$ leptons, neutrinos and jets.

The $U_{1}$ leptoquark transforms as
$U_{1}^{\mu}\sim(3,1,2/3)$
under the SM gauge group.
It is therefore a colour-triplet, weak-singlet vector boson with hypercharge $Y=2/3$. 
Since it is an $SU(2)$ singlet, its electric charge is $Q=T^{3}+Y=2/3$.

A commonly used simplified-model Lagrangian~\cite{Cornella2019} for $U_{1}$ is
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_{U}
    =
    &
    -\frac{1}{2}
    U_{1\mu\nu}^{\dagger}
    U_{1}^{\mu\nu}
    +
    \MU^{2}
    U_{1\mu}^{\dagger}
    U_{1}^{\mu}
    \nonumber\\
    &
    -ig_s(1-\kappa_{c})
    U_{1\mu}^{\dagger}
    T^{a}
    U_{1\nu}
    G^{a\mu\nu}
    -
    i\frac{2}{3}g'(1-\kappa_{Y})
    U_{1\mu}^{\dagger}
    U_{1\nu}
    B^{\mu\nu}
    \nonumber\\
    &
    +
    \left(
        U_{1}^{\mu}J_{\mu}
        +
        \mathrm{h.c.}
    \right),
    \label{eq:u1_full_lagrangian}
\end{align}
where $U_{1}^{\mu}$ is the vector leptoquark field and $\MU$ is the leptoquark mass. 
The first term is the kinetic term, while the second term is the mass term. 
The field-strength tensor of $U_{1}$ is defined as
\begin{align*}
    U_{1\mu\nu}
    =
    D_{\mu}U_{1\nu}
    -
    D_{\nu}U_{1\mu},
\end{align*}
with the covariant derivative
\begin{align*}
    D_{\mu}
    =
    \partial_{\mu}
    -
    ig_sG_{\mu}^{a}T^{a}
    -
    i\frac{2}{3}g'B_{\mu},
\end{align*}
where $G_{\mu}^{a}$, $B_{\mu}$, $g_s$ and $g'$ are the SM gauge fields and couplings of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sm_covariant_derivative}, and $T^{a}=\lambda^{a}/2$ are the $SU(3)_{C}$ generators. 

The parameters $\kappa_{c}$ and $\kappa_{Y}$ control the interactions
of the vector leptoquark with the SM gauge fields.
$\kappa_{c}=\kappa_{Y}=0$ corresponds to the Yang--Mills scenario,
whereas $\kappa_{c}=\kappa_{Y}=1$ corresponds to the minimal-coupling
scenario~\cite{Baker2019HighPTU1}.
The Yang--Mills scenario is the one obtained when $U_{1}$ is a gauge boson of a spontaneously broken extended symmetry, as in the 4321 model, and is adopted throughout this thesis.

The last term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:u1_full_lagrangian} describes the interaction of $U_{1}$ with SM fermions through the current $J_{\mu}$. It is given by
\begin{align}
    J_{\mu}
    =
    \frac{\gU}{\sqrt{2}}
    \left[
        \beta_{L}^{i\alpha}
        \left(
            \bar{q}_{L}^{i}
            \gamma_{\mu}
            \ell_{L}^{\alpha}
        \right)
        +
        \beta_{R}^{i\alpha}
        \left(
            \bar{d}_{R}^{i}
            \gamma_{\mu}
            e_{R}^{\alpha}
        \right)
    \right],
    \label{eq:u1_fermion_current}
\end{align}
where $i=1,2,3$ labels the quark generation and $\alpha=1,2,3$ labels the lepton generation. $q_{L}^{i}$ and $\ell_{L}^{\alpha}$ are left-handed quark and lepton doublets, while $d_{R}^{i}$ and $e_{R}^{\alpha}$ are right-handed down-type quark and charged-lepton singlets. 
The parameter $\gU$ denotes the overall strength of the $U_{1}$ coupling to fermions. 
The matrices $\beta_{L}^{i\alpha}$ and $\beta_{R}^{i\alpha}$ are complex $3\times3$ flavour-coupling matrices for the left- and right-handed interactions, respectively. 
The strength of a given flavour coupling is therefore proportional to $\gU\beta_{L,R}^{i\alpha}$.

The down-quark and charged-lepton mass eigenstate basis is adopted for the $SU(2)$ multiplets:
\begin{align*}
    q_{L}^{i}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        V_{ji}^{*}u_{L}^{j}\\
        d_{L}^{i}
    \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    \ell_{L}^{\alpha}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \nu_{L}^{\alpha}\\
        e_{L}^{\alpha}
    \end{pmatrix},
\end{align*}
where $V$ is the CKM matrix. 

Expanding the left-handed current from Eq.~\eqref{eq:u1_fermion_current} gives
\begin{align}
    \bar{q}_{L}^{i}
    \gamma_{\mu}
    \ell_{L}^{\alpha}
    =
    V_{ji}
    \bar{u}_{L}^{j}
    \gamma_{\mu}
    \nu_{L}^{\alpha}
    +
    \bar{d}_{L}^{i}
    \gamma_{\mu}
    e_{L}^{\alpha}.
    \label{eq:u1_current_expanded}
\end{align}
This expression shows that a single left-handed flavour coupling $\beta_{L}^{i\alpha}$ gives two related interactions. 
One involves an up-type quark together with a neutrino, while the other involves a down-type quark together with a charged lepton.

The simplified $U_{1}$ model allows general flavour couplings to the three quark and lepton generations~\cite{Baker2019HighPTU1}. This thesis focuses on the charged-current $b\rightarrow c\tau\nu$ transition and on signatures containing a $\tau$ lepton and a neutrino. The couplings to leptons of the first two generations are therefore set to zero, and only the couplings to third-generation leptons are retained. The flavour-coupling matrices are taken to be
\begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
        \beta_{L} &=
        \begin{pmatrix}
            0 & 0 & \beta_{L}^{13} \\
            0 & 0 & \beta_{L}^{23} \\
            0 & 0 & \beta_{L}^{33}
        \end{pmatrix},
        \qquad
        \beta_{R} =
        \begin{pmatrix}
            0 & 0 & 0 \\
            0 & 0 & 0 \\
            0 & 0 & \beta_{R}^{33}
        \end{pmatrix}.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The third-generation coupling is normalised to $\beta_{L}^{33}=1$, while $\gU$ remains a free parameter controlling the overall fermionic coupling strength. Under the assumption that the LQ couplings to the first two quark generations follow the CKM mixing pattern, $\beta_L^{13}=(V_{td}^{*}/V_{ts}^{*})\beta_L^{23}$, giving $|\beta_L^{13}|\simeq0.2|\beta_L^{23}|$~\cite{Aebischer2022}.

Within this benchmark, Eq.~\eqref{eq:u1_current_expanded} shows that $\beta_{L}^{23}$ controls the $s\tau$ coupling directly and the $c\nu_\tau$ coupling through $V_{cs}\beta_L^{23}$, while $\beta_L^{33}$ controls the $b\tau$ coupling directly and contributes $V_{cb}\beta_L^{33}$ to the $c\nu_\tau$ coupling.


The heavy $U_{1}$ field can be integrated out at the matching scale $\mu\sim \MU$.
At tree level, its exchange generates four-fermion operators involving left- and right-handed quark and lepton fields~\cite{Aebischer2022}. The corresponding effective Lagrangian can be written as
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{U}
=
-\frac{2}{v^{2}}
\bigg[
& C_{LL}^{ij\alpha\beta}
\mathcal{O}_{LL}^{ij\alpha\beta}
+
C_{RR}^{ij\alpha\beta}
\mathcal{O}_{RR}^{ij\alpha\beta}
+
\left(
C_{LR}^{ij\alpha\beta}
\mathcal{O}_{LR}^{ij\alpha\beta}
+
\mathrm{h.c.}
\right)
\bigg].
\label{eq:u1_low_energy_effective_lagrangian}
\end{align}

The four-fermion operators are defined as
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{O}_{LL}^{ij\alpha\beta}
&=
\left(
\bar{q}_{L}^{i}\gamma_{\mu}\ell_{L}^{\alpha}
\right)
\left(
\bar{\ell}_{L}^{\beta}\gamma^{\mu}q_{L}^{j}
\right),
\\
\mathcal{O}_{RR}^{ij\alpha\beta}
&=
\left(
\bar{d}_{R}^{i}\gamma_{\mu}e_{R}^{\alpha}
\right)
\left(
\bar{e}_{R}^{\beta}\gamma^{\mu}d_{R}^{j}
\right),
\\
\mathcal{O}_{LR}^{ij\alpha\beta}
&=
\left(
\bar{q}_{L}^{i}\gamma_{\mu}\ell_{L}^{\alpha}
\right)
\left(
\bar{e}_{R}^{\beta}\gamma^{\mu}d_{R}^{j}
\right).
\end{align*}

The Wilson coefficients are
\begin{align*}
C_{LL}^{ij\alpha\beta}
&=
C_{U}\beta_{L}^{i\alpha}
\left(
\beta_{L}^{j\beta}
\right)^{*},
\\
C_{RR}^{ij\alpha\beta}
&=
C_{U}\beta_{R}^{i\alpha}
\left(
\beta_{R}^{j\beta}
\right)^{*},
\\
C_{LR}^{ij\alpha\beta}
&=
C_{U}\beta_{L}^{i\alpha}
\left(
\beta_{R}^{j\beta}
\right)^{*}.
\end{align*}

The overall coefficient is defined as
\begin{align*}
C_{U}
&=
\frac{\gU^{2}v^{2}}{4\MU^{2}},
\end{align*}
where $v\simeq246~\GeV$ is the Higgs vacuum expectation value.
The coefficient $C_{U}$ sets the overall strength of the effective interaction and scales as $\gU^{2}/\MU^{2}$, while the flavour dependence is encoded in the coupling matrices $\beta_{L}$ and $\beta_{R}$.

From Eq.~\eqref{eq:u1_low_energy_effective_lagrangian}, the effective Lagrangian for the $b\to c\tau\nu$ transition can be written as:
\begin{align*}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{b\rightarrow c\tau\nu}
    =
    -\frac{4G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}V_{cb}
    \bigg[
        &
        \left(
            1+C_{LL}^{c}
        \right)
        \left(
            \bar{c}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}b_{L}
        \right)
        \left(
            \bar{\tau}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}\nu_{\tau L}
        \right)
        \nonumber\\
        &
        -
        2C_{LR}^{c}
        \left(
            \bar{c}_{L}b_{R}
        \right)
        \left(
            \bar{\tau}_{R}\nu_{\tau L}
        \right)
    \bigg],
\end{align*}
where $G_{F}=1/(\sqrt{2}v^{2})$ is the Fermi constant.
The coefficient $C_{LL}^{c}$ modifies the SM-like left-handed vector
interaction, while $C_{LR}^{c}$ is associated with the scalar operator
arising from the combination of left- and right-handed fermion couplings
of the $U_{1}$ leptoquark.

For the flavour structure introduced above, the corresponding coefficients
are
\begin{align*}
    C_{LL}^{c}
    &=
    C_{U}
    \frac{
        \left(\beta_{L}^{33}\right)^{*}
    }{
        V_{cb}
    }
    \left(
        V_{cd}\beta_{L}^{13}
        +
        V_{cs}\beta_{L}^{23}
        +
        V_{cb}\beta_{L}^{33}
    \right),
    \\
    C_{LR}^{c}
    &=
    C_{U}
    \frac{
        \left(\beta_{R}^{33}\right)^{*}
    }{
        V_{cb}
    }
    \left(
        V_{cd}\beta_{L}^{13}
        +
        V_{cs}\beta_{L}^{23}
        +
        V_{cb}\beta_{L}^{33}
    \right).
\end{align*}

Using the normalisation $\beta_{L}^{33}=1$ and neglecting the small
first-generation coupling $\beta_{L}^{13}$, the coefficients reduce to
\begin{align}
    C_{LL}^{c}
    &\simeq
    C_{U}
    \left(
        1+
        \frac{V_{cs}}{V_{cb}}\beta_{L}^{23}
    \right),
    \label{eq:cll_c_approx}
    \\
    C_{LR}^{c}
    &\simeq
    C_{U}
    \left(\beta_{R}^{33}\right)^{*}
    \left(
        1+
        \frac{V_{cs}}{V_{cb}}\beta_{L}^{23}
    \right).
    \label{eq:clr_c_approx}
\end{align}

% associated with the scalar operator.

\subsection{Low-energy fit and benchmark scenarios}
\label{subsec:u1_low_energy_fit}

The Wilson coefficients $C_{LL}^{c}$ and $C_{LR}^{c}$ introduced above can be constrained using measurements of semileptonic charged-current $B$-meson decays. Ref.~\cite{Aebischer2022} performed a combined fit to low-energy $b\rightarrow c\tau\nu$ observables (e.g., $R(D^{(*)})$ and $R(\Lambda_c)$) and determined the preferred region in the $(C_{LL}^{c},C_{LR}^{c})$ plane. 
The fit result is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:cll_clr_low_energy_fit}. The Wilson coefficients in this figure are evolved to a common reference scale of $1~\TeV$ to facilitate comparison with high-energy observables. The best-fit point is $(C_{LL}^{c}, C_{LR}^{c})=(0.05,-0.02)$, and the preferred region is compatible with both pure-left-handed ($\beta_{R}^{33}=0$) and sizeable right-handed ($\beta_{R}^{33}=-1$) scenarios.
The same study also found that LFU tests in $\tau$ decays can strongly constrain the pure-left-handed explanation of $R(D^{(*)})$.
However, additional particles in UV completions may weaken these constraints.
This analysis therefore retains both benchmark scenarios without applying these model-dependent constraints.
In addition, although the sign of $\beta_R^{33}$ affects the low-energy relation between $C_{LL}^{c}$ and $C_{LR}^{c}$, the collider predictions used in this thesis are identical for $\beta_R^{33}=+1$ and $-1$. At collider energies, interference terms sensitive to the sign of $\beta_R^{33}$ are negligible. The collider interpretation therefore depends only on $|\beta_R^{33}|$.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.74\textwidth]{Figs/CH3/CLL_CLR_lowPT_HFLAV2023.pdf}

\paragraph{Pure-left-handed scenario.}
For $\beta_{R}^{33}=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:clr_c_approx} gives $C_{LR}^{c}=0$.  Therefore, the $U_{1}$ leptoquark contribution to $b\rightarrow c\tau\nu$ is described only by the left-handed vector operator, as given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:cll_c_approx}. This is the primary benchmark scenario considered in this thesis. The corresponding $B$-anomaly-favoured region can be obtained from the intersection of the low-energy fit with the line $C_{LR}^{c}=0$ in Figure~\ref{fig:cll_clr_low_energy_fit}.

\paragraph{Right-handed scenario.}
The second benchmark is obtained by allowing a sizeable right-handed third-generation coupling. For $\beta_{R}^{33}=-1$, the relation between the Wilson coefficients becomes
\begin{align*}
    C_{LR}^{c}=-C_{LL}^{c}.
\end{align*}
This benchmark therefore corresponds to the diagonal line in Figure~\ref{fig:cll_clr_low_energy_fit}, where the right-handed third-generation coupling has the same magnitude as the left-handed coupling.

Both scenarios are parametrised by $(\MU,\gU,\beta_L^{23})$, with $\beta_R^{33}$ fixed to $0$ or $-1$. The low-energy $b\rightarrow c\tau\nu$ data constrain only the combination $C_{U}(1+V_{cs}\beta_L^{23}/V_{cb})$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cll_c_approx}, where $C_{U}\propto\gU^{2}/\MU^{2}$. A measured value of $C_{LL}^{c}$ therefore defines a curve in the $(\gU/\MU,\beta_L^{23})$ plane rather than a point, and low-energy data alone do not determine $\beta_L^{23}$. Along this curve a larger $\beta_L^{23}$ is compensated by a smaller $\gU/\MU$, with the CKM ratio of $V_{cs}/V_{cb}\approx24$.

Theoretical constraints on $\beta_L^{23}$ arise instead from possible UV completions of the simplified $U_{1}$ model, such as the 4321 model introduced in Section~\ref{subsec:leptoquarks_motivation}. In the flavour structure considered in this model, the $U_1$ couplings are non-universal, with $|\beta_{L}^{23}|<|\beta_{L}^{33}|$~\cite{Cornella2019}.

Additional constraints can be introduced when considering $\Delta F=2$ observables. These are processes in which a quark-flavour quantum number changes by two units, including neutral-meson mixing such as $B_s$--$\bar{B}_s$, $K$--$\bar{K}$, and $D$--$\bar{D}$ mixing. In a given UV completion, the flavour-symmetry breaking that generates non-universal $U_{1}$ couplings may also be transmitted to the neutral-current sector, inducing flavour-changing neutral currents (FCNC) and consequently additional contributions to $\Delta F=2$ processes. The strong experimental constraints on neutral-meson mixing therefore restrict the allowed flavour-violating structure of the UV model and indirectly constrain $\beta_{L}^{23}$.

Combining such considerations of the UV flavour structure with constraints from $\Delta F=2$ observables, Ref.~\cite{Aebischer2022} identifies
\begin{align*}
\beta_{L}^{23}\in[0.06,0.16]
\end{align*} as a theoretically motivated range for experimental searches. However, this constraint should not be interpreted as a model-independent experimental bound on $\beta_{L}^{23}$. Additional particles, flavour symmetries, or cancellation mechanisms may suppress flavour-changing transitions and thereby allow larger values of $\beta_{L}^{23}$. Therefore, large values of $\beta_{L}^{23}$ remain a possibility within the $U_{1}$ model, and it is important to test this complementary region experimentally.

\section{Leptoquark Searches at the LHC}
\label{sec:lq_searches_lhc}

\subsection{Production modes and existing searches}
\label{subsec:lq_searches_lhc}

Leptoquarks can be searched for at the LHC through several production modes, including pair production, resonant single production, and non-resonant $t$-channel exchange, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:lq_production_modes}. Pair production is dominated by QCD interactions. For a fixed choice of $\kappa_c$, as defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:u1_full_lagrangian}, its cross-section depends strongly on $\MU$ but weakly on the couplings $\gU$ and $\beta^{i\alpha}$. Pair-production searches therefore provide relatively robust constraints on the leptoquark mass.

ATLAS and CMS have performed searches for pair-produced leptoquarks coupling
to third-generation fermions in final states containing $\tau$ leptons,
$b$-jets, and, depending on the decay mode, missing transverse momentum~\cite{ATLAS2023LQBTau,CMS2021LQThirdGen}.
For the vector $U_{1}$, the mass limit extends to approximately
$1.5$--$1.8~\TeV$ as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:lq_atlas_summary_u1}. The limit also depends on the modelling choice of the $U_{1}$--gluon coupling, Yang--Mills or minimal coupling, as well as on the decay pattern determined by the assumed fermionic couplings.

For single production, the cross-section also depends strongly on the leptoquark--fermion couplings. Searches for singly produced leptoquarks decaying into $b\tau$ have been performed in the $b\tau\tau$ final state, where the leptoquark is produced in association with a $\tau$ lepton as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:lq_single_production}~\cite{CMS2024LQBTau}.

In addition, virtual $t$-channel $U_1$ exchange can contribute to final states such as $\tau\tau$ or $\tau\nu$, enhancing the event yields in the high-mass region. ATLAS and CMS have therefore searched for non-resonant leptoquark contributions in high-mass $\tau\tau$ production~\cite{ATLAS2025HighMassTauTau,CMS2023TauTauLQ}. These searches can remain sensitive to masses beyond the reach of the direct pair-production searches, particularly for large $\gU$, since the sensitivity is approximately governed by $\gU/\MU$.

In the non-resonant $\tau\tau$ process shown in Figure~\ref{fig:lq_tchannel_exchange}, both leptoquark vertices involve a $b$ quark and a $\tau$ lepton and the coupling strength is scaled by $\gU\beta_L^{33}$ with fixed $\beta_L^{33}=1$ and $\beta_L^{23}=0.19$~\cite{CMS2023TauTauLQ}.
Consequently, the $c\nu_{\tau}$ coupling, and in particular the large-$\beta_L^{23}$ region, has not been systematically explored at the LHC.

However, as discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:u1_low_energy_fit}, experimental constraints still allow scenarios with large values of $\beta_L^{23}$.
This motivates a search in the $\tau\nu(+b)$ final state, which can be produced through non-resonant $t$-channel LQ exchange involving both the $b\tau$ and $c\nu_\tau$ couplings. 
CMS has performed an inclusive search for events with a $\hadtau$ and large missing transverse momentum, including an interpretation in terms of leptoquark exchange, but without an explicit $b$-jet requirement~\cite{CMS2023TauNu}, and no dedicated ATLAS or CMS leptoquark search had targeted the $\tau\nu(+b)$ topology before this analysis. The dedicated $\tau\nu(+b)$ search presented in this thesis is therefore complementary to the existing $\tau\tau(+b)$ and $\tau\nu$ searches, since it probes both the $b\tau$ and $c\nu_{\tau}$ couplings and extends the collider interpretation to large values of $\beta_{L}^{23}$. The corresponding benchmark and target signatures are introduced in the following subsection.

\subsection{Signal model and target signatures}
\label{subsec:lq_collider_benchmark}

The signal model is the simplified $U_{1}$ model of Section~\ref{subsec:u1_simplified_model} in the Yang--Mills scenario with $\beta_{L}^{33}=1$, in the two benchmark scenarios of Section~\ref{subsec:u1_low_energy_fit}. The pure-left-handed scenario ($\beta_{R}^{33}=0$) is the primary interpretation, and the right-handed scenario ($\beta_{R}^{33}=-1$) is presented with the final results. The signal is therefore characterised by $(\MU,\gU,\beta_L^{23})$ for each fixed value of $\beta_R^{33}$.
For the pure-left-handed interpretation, $\beta_L^{23}$ is scanned over $0\leq\beta_L^{23}\leq2.2$, as described in Section~\ref{sec:signal_samples}.

Figure~\ref{fig:u1_taunu_target_topologies} shows representative $U_{1}$ signal diagrams targeted by the analysis. Both non-resonant $t$-channel exchange and resonant single production are considered, and each is divided according to the presence or absence of a $b$-jet. This results in four target signatures: non-resonant $\tau\nu+b$, non-resonant $\tau\nu+0b$, resonant $\tau\nu+b$, and resonant $\tau\nu+0b$.

The $b$-jet requirement provides strong discrimination against the dominant SM $\tau\nu+\mathrm{jets}$ background, since SM production of a $\tau\nu$ system in association with a $b$-quark is CKM suppressed~\cite{Endo2022NonResonant}. The $\tau\nu+b$ categories therefore have a small SM background and probe the product of the $b\tau$ and $c\nu_{\tau}$ couplings.

The zero-$b$ categories extend the sensitivity to the large $\beta_{L}^{23}$ region explored in this thesis. As $\beta_{L}^{23}$ increases, the $s\tau$ and $c\nu_{\tau}$ interactions also become sizeable, producing signal events without a final-state $b$-quark. This contribution is particularly relevant for resonant production, where an on-shell $U_{1}$ can decay into $s\tau$ or $c\nu_{\tau}$. The four categories therefore provide complementary sensitivity to the different flavour and production configurations of the $U_{1}$ signal.
