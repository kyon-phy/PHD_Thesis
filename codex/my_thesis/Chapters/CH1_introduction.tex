\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chapter1:introduction}

Particle physics studies the elementary constituents of matter and the fundamental interactions between them.
At present, the most successful theoretical description of particle physics is the Standard Model~(SM), a quantum field theory based on the gauge symmetry $SU(3)_{C} \times SU(2)_{L} \times U(1)_{Y}$.
In this framework, matter consists of three generations of quarks and leptons, and the strong, weak and electromagnetic interactions between them are mediated by the gauge bosons associated with this symmetry.
The masses of the weak gauge bosons and the fermions are generated by the Higgs mechanism, in which the electroweak symmetry $SU(2)_{L} \times U(1)_{Y}$ is spontaneously broken by the Higgs field.
This mechanism predicts a scalar particle, the Higgs boson, which was discovered by the ATLAS and CMS experiments in 2012~\cite{ATLAS2012Higgs,CMS2012Higgs}.

The SM has been tested with remarkable precision over a wide range of energy scales from the MeV to the TeV scale, and has successfully described a vast number of experimental observations. 
Nevertheless, it is widely regarded as an incomplete theory. 
It does not provide a particle candidate for dark matter, does not explain the observed matter--antimatter asymmetry of the universe, and does not account for the origin of non-zero neutrino masses. 
In addition, the pattern of fermion masses and flavour mixing remains unexplained, and the large separation between the electroweak and Planck scales suggests the possible existence of new physics beyond the SM. 
These open questions motivate direct searches for new particles and interactions.

Among the many possible extensions of the SM, this thesis focuses on leptoquarks~(LQs), hypothetical bosons that couple directly to a quark and a lepton.
They appear in grand unified theories and in models of quark--lepton unification.
Leptoquarks are also of particular phenomenological interest because they can contribute to $b\to c\tau\nu$ transitions.
Such contributions can explain the observed anomalies in semileptonic $B$-meson decays.
The benchmark model considered in this thesis is the vector singlet leptoquark $U_{1}$, which contributes to these transitions through its couplings to $b\tau$ and $c\nu_\tau$.

The same couplings produce a $\tau$ lepton and a neutrino in proton--proton~($pp$) collisions at the Large Hadron Collider~(LHC), through non-resonant $t$-channel $U_{1}$ exchange or resonant single production.
This thesis therefore presents a search for leptoquarks in events with a hadronically decaying $\tau$ lepton ($\hadtau$), large missing transverse momentum ($\met$) from the neutrinos, and jets.
The search uses $140~\ifb$ of $pp$ collision data recorded by the ATLAS detector during Run~2 (2015--2018) at a centre-of-mass energy of $\sqrt{s}=13~\TeV$.
