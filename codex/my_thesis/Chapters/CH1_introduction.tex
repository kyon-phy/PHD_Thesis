\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chapter1:introduction}
\section{Introduction}

Modern particle physics seeks to understand the elementary constituents of matter and the fundamental interactions that govern their behaviour. 
At present, the most successful theoretical description of particle physics is the Standard Model~(SM), a quantum field theory based on the gauge symmetry $SU(3)_{C} \times SU(2)_{L} \times U(1)_{Y}$.
Within this framework, matter is described by three generations of quarks and leptons, while the electromagnetic, weak, and strong interactions are mediated by gauge bosons. 
The electroweak symmetry is spontaneously broken through the Higgs mechanism, giving masses to the weak gauge bosons and to the fermions through their interactions with the Higgs field. 
The Higgs boson was discovered by the ATLAS and CMS experiments in 2012.

The SM has been tested with remarkable precision over a wide range of energy scales from the MeV to the TeV scale, and has successfully described a vast number of experimental observations. 
Nevertheless, it is widely regarded as an incomplete theory. 
It does not provide a particle candidate for dark matter, does not explain the observed matter--antimatter asymmetry of the universe, and does not account for the origin of non-zero neutrino masses. 
In addition, the pattern of fermion masses and flavour mixing remains unexplained, and the large separation between the electroweak and Planck scales suggests the possible existence of new physics beyond the SM. 
These open questions motivate direct searches for new particles and interactions.

Among the many possible extensions of the SM, this thesis considers leptoquarks~(LQs), hypothetical bosons that couple directly to a quark and a lepton.
They appear in grand unified theories and in models of quark--lepton unification.
Leptoquarks are also of particular phenomenological interest because they can modify $B$-meson decays.

A search for leptoquarks is presented in this thesis using $140~\ifb$ of proton--proton~($pp$) collision data recorded by the ATLAS detector at the Large Hadron Collider~(LHC) during Run~2 (2015--2018) at a centre-of-mass energy of $\sqrt{s}=13~\TeV$. 
This thesis focuses on the vector singlet leptoquark $U_{1}$ as the benchmark model, as it can contribute to $b\to c\tau\nu$ transitions.
These contributions can explain the observed anomalies in semileptonic $B$-meson decays.
Therefore, the analysis targets events with a hadronically decaying $\tau$ lepton ($\hadtau$), large missing transverse momentum ($\met$), and jets. 

This dissertation is organised as follows. 
Chapter 2 provides an overview of the SM's theoretical framework and the physics beyond it.
Chapter 3 describes the leptoquark models and the searches performed at the LHC. 
Chapter 4 introduces the LHC and the ATLAS detector.
Chapter 5 describes the dataset and simulated samples.
Chapter 6 details the reconstruction and the identification of the physics objects in ATLAS.
Chapter 7 discusses the analysis strategy and event selections for the leptoquark search.
Chapter 8 presents the background estimation strategy and method.
Chapter 9 discusses the systematic uncertainties for the signal and background.
Chapter 10 introduces the statistical method, presents the results and discusses the interpretations.
Chapter 11 summarises this analysis.
