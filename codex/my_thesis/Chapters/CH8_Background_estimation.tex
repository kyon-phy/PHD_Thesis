Since the fake-$\hadtau$ background is small, a simulation-based method is used for background estimation, and several regions are defined for this purpose.
Control regions (CRs) are used to correct background mismodelling, and validation regions (VRs) are used to test the background predictions.
This chapter presents the background estimation strategy, the CR and VR definitions, and the validation of the background predictions obtained with the background-only fit introduced in Section~\ref{subsec:ch10_fit_strategy}.

\section{Background estimation strategy}
\label{sec:ch8_strategy}

To predict the background yields in the SRs accurately, a maximum-likelihood fit as described in Section~\ref{sec:ch10_statistical_model} is used with dedicated CRs defined for the dominant backgrounds.
These regions are enriched with the corresponding SM background and have small signal contamination. They are used to determine free normalisation factors (NFs) for the dominant backgrounds.

Two fit configurations are used in this analysis as described in Section~\ref{subsec:ch10_fit_strategy}.
A background-only fit uses data in the CRs alone to obtain the background predictions, while a signal-plus-background fit uses data in both the CRs and SRs to test the signal hypothesis.
In both fits, NFs are determined by fitting the simulated predictions to the observed event yields.
Each NF scales the simulated yield of the corresponding background and is varied together with the nuisance parameters.
The NFs are also determined independently for the resonant and non-resonant regions, which means different normalisations in these regions are allowed while fitting since these regions are kinematically different and may require different corrections.
The fitted NFs are then applied to the simulated yields to obtain the expected background yields in the SRs.

The CRs are therefore designed to have event topologies similar to those of the SRs so that the NFs determined in the CRs can be extrapolated to the SRs with minimal uncertainty.
On the other hand, the final states in CRs must differ from those of the SRs to keep the signal contamination small.
For example, CRs for $\wjets$ require a light lepton instead of a $\hadtau$, and CRs for the top-quark background require an additional light lepton in place of the second $\tau$ lepton that is reconstructed as a jet in the SRs.
Because of these differences, the NFs determined in the CRs may not describe the backgrounds in the SRs exactly, which may introduce additional uncertainties when the NFs are extrapolated to these regions.
Like the other systematic uncertainties, these uncertainties are represented by nuisance parameters with corresponding constraint terms in the likelihood function.
In this analysis, an additional uncertainty in the heavy-to-light-flavour ratio is assigned when extrapolating from the $b$-vetoed CRs to SRs with $b$-jets, as discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:ch9_wjets}.

The VRs are used to test the background estimation strategy by comparing the observed event yields with predictions obtained using the NFs and nuisance parameters determined by the background-only fit to the CRs.
The VR data are not included in the likelihood fit.
These regions are therefore also designed to have a similar topology to the SRs and to have small signal contamination.

The estimation strategy for each background, including the definitions of the CRs and VRs, is summarised below:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\wjets$ is estimated using NFs constrained by data in $\CRW$, where one light lepton (electron or muon) and no $\hadtau$ or $b$-jets are required.
    The $0b\to1b$ extrapolation is tested in $\VRW$, where one light lepton, no $\hadtau$ and one $b$-jet are required. This is explained in Section~\ref{sec:ch8_wjets}.

    \item The top-quark background is estimated using NFs constrained by data in $\CRTop$, where one $\hadtau$, one light lepton and at least one $b$-jet are required.
    The prediction is tested in $\VRTop$, where one $\hadtau$, no light leptons and two $b$-jets are required. This is explained in Section~\ref{sec:ch8_top}.

    \item $\znunu$ with a jet misidentified as a $\hadtau$ is estimated directly from simulation.
    A conservative uncertainty is assigned based on the study of $\zll$ events with a fake-$\hadtau$ described in Appendix~\ref{app:ch8_fake_tau}.

    \item $\ztautau$ and diboson production are estimated directly from simulation without dedicated CRs.
    Their predicted yields are allowed to vary within conservative uncertainties in the fit.

    \item The multijet background is negligible after the full SR selections, including the suppression described in Section~\ref{subsec:ch7_baseline_selection}, as checked in Appendix~\ref{App:QCD_bkg_study}.
\end{itemize}

Four CRs are therefore defined in this analysis: $\CRW$-$\Res$ and $\CRW$-$\NonRes$ for $\wjets$, and $\CRTop$-$\Res$ and $\CRTop$-$\NonRes$ for the top-quark background, corresponding to the normalisation factors $\lambda_W^{\Res}$, $\lambda_W^{\NonRes}$, $\lambda_t^{\Res}$ and $\lambda_t^{\NonRes}$, respectively. They are defined as follows:
\begin{equation*}
    \lambda_{bg} = \frac{N_{bg}^{\mathrm{CR,data}}}{N_{bg}^{\mathrm{CR,MC}}},
\end{equation*}
where $N_{bg}^{\mathrm{CR,data}}$ and $N_{bg}^{\mathrm{CR,MC}}$ are the observed and the predicted background event yields in the CR, respectively.

Regarding the top-quark ($\wjets$) contribution in $\CRW$ ($\CRTop$), it amounts to less than $2\%$ ($5\%$) of the total yield in $\CRW$ ($\CRTop$).
Even a $50\%$ uncertainty in either contribution corresponds to a variation of less than $2.5\%$ in the total yield in the corresponding CR.
The uncertainties in these small contributions are expected to have only a small effect on the NFs determined from the CRs.
No additional uncertainties are therefore assigned specifically to these small contributions in the likelihood fit.

\section[W+jets]{$\wjets$}
\label{sec:ch8_wjets}

The $\CRW$ regions are defined by replacing the $\tau$ lepton in the SR selection with a light lepton, targeting the $W\to\ell\nu$ process. Here $\ell=e$ or $\mu$.
The $b$-jet veto suppresses the top-quark background and increases the $\wjets$ event purity.
Single-lepton triggers are used, as listed in Table~\ref{tab:run2_trigger_chains}.
This avoids differences in the $\met$ trigger response between the electron and muon channels, since the muon momentum is not included in the trigger-level $\met$ calculation.
$\mT^\ell$ and $\DphiLepMet$ are calculated using the light lepton as a proxy for the $\tau$ lepton, and the leading jet is used as a proxy for the $b$-jet. The other selections are the same as those in the SRs.

The definitions of the $\CRW$ and $\VRW$ regions are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:ch8_wcr_vrw}.
After the background-only fit, the $\wjets$ purity is approximately $95\%$ in $\CRW$-$\NonRes$ and $82\%$ in $\CRW$-$\Res$. Diboson events are expected to contribute approximately $14\%$ in $\CRW$-$\Res$.
The additional neutrino in a hadronic $\tau$ decay introduces kinematic differences in the $1\ell\to1\hadtau$ extrapolation. The prediction is further tested in the lower-mass $1\hadtau0\ell1b$ regions described in Appendix~\ref{app:ch8_vrw_excess}, which check the extrapolation to events with both a $\hadtau$ and a $b$-jet.

The signal contamination is checked with samples including both hadronic and leptonic $\tau$ decays at parameter points $(\MU,\gU,\beta_L^{23})=(1.5~\TeV,1.5,0.2)$ and $(2.5~\TeV,1.5,1.0)$ for the resonant and non-resonant CRs, respectively. The contamination is found to be below $1\%$ in both regions.

The $\CRW$ $N-1$ distributions after the background-only fit are shown separately for the electron and muon channels in Figures~\ref{fig:ch8_wcr_res_e}--\ref{fig:ch8_wcr_nonres_mu}.
There are 243 electron and 219 muon events in $\CRW$-$\Res$, and 474 electron and 398 muon events in $\CRW$-$\NonRes$.
No clear slope is observed in the data/bkg. ratios, except in the jet-$\pT$ distribution in Figure~\ref{fig:ch8_wcr_nonres_mu}(\subref{fig:ch8_wcr_nonres_mu_jetpt}).
The average effect of this jet-$\pT$ dependence in each region could be absorbed by the independent $\wjets$ normalisation factors.

The difference between the data/bkg. ratios of the electron and muon channels is consistent with statistical fluctuations, as shown in Appendix~\ref{app:ch8_emu}.
The two channels are therefore combined.

\section[Top-quark background]{Top-quark background}
\label{sec:ch8_top}

The $\CRTop$ regions are defined by replacing the light-lepton veto in the SR selection with exactly one light lepton to correct the modelling through $\ttbar\to b\tau\nu\,\bar b\ell\nu$ and $Wt\to\ell\nu\,b\tau\nu$ processes.
Since few top-quark background events pass the high-$\mT$ requirement, single-lepton triggers are used to allow a lower $\met$ threshold than in the SRs and increase the CR yields.

To further increase the CR yields, at least one $b$-jet is required instead of exactly one. The shape comparison in Appendix~\ref{app:ch8_topcr_1b2b} supports combining the one- and two-$b$-jet samples. The $\pT^{\hadtau}$ and $\mT$ thresholds are also lowered, and no $m_{b\hadtau}$ requirement is applied.
The leading $b$-jet is used to calculate the $b$-related variables in case multiple $b$-jets are selected.
The other selections are the same as the ones in SRs.

The $\CRTop$ definition is summarised in Table~\ref{tab:ch8_top_regions}.
The top-quark background purity is approximately $90\%$ in both $\CRTop$ regions.
Signal contamination is negligible because the additional light lepton is not expected in the signal topology.

\section{Background-only fit and validation}
\label{sec:ch8_validation}

The background-only fit is performed on the CRs to obtain the background normalisation factors.
Both statistical and systematic uncertainties are included in the fit. The fit model and systematic uncertainties are described in detail in Section~\ref{sec:ch10_statistical_model} and Chapter~\ref{chapter:systematics_uncertainties}, respectively.
The four fitted NFs are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:ch8_normalisation}, in which $\lambda_W^{\Res}$ and $\lambda_W^{\NonRes}$ have opposite scaling directions. This difference could arise from the jet-$\pT$ dependence of the $\wjets$ mismodelling as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:ch8_wjets}.

\subsection[VRW]{$\VRW$}
\label{subsec:ch8_vrw}

As in the SRs, the background in $\VRW$ is dominated by $\wjets$, which contributes approximately $67\%$ ($68\%$) in the resonant (non-resonant) region after the background-only fit.
Top-quark and diboson production contribute approximately $23\%$ ($29\%$) and $9.5\%$ ($2.9\%$), respectively.

The signal contamination is checked using the samples introduced in Section~\ref{sec:ch8_wjets}.
% Direct signal production with an electron or muon is suppressed by the small couplings to these leptons, while hadronic $\tau$ decays contribute little to the selected sample.
The contribution from leptonic $\tau$ decays is $(1.3\pm0.1)\%$ in $\VRW$-$\Res$ and $(0.7\pm0.1)\%$ in $\VRW$-$\NonRes$, while the contribution from hadronic $\tau$ decays is negligible.

After the background-only fit, the $\VRW$-$\Res$ yield agrees with the prediction within about $1\sigma$, while a deviation of about $2\sigma$ is observed in $\VRW$-$\NonRes$, as shown in Figures~\ref{fig:ch8_vrw_res} and~\ref{fig:ch8_vrw_nonres}.

The finer-binned $\met$ distribution in Figure~\ref{fig:ch8_vrw_met_study}(\subref{fig:ch8_vrw_met_study_met}) shows that the excess is concentrated in $425<\met<450~\GeV$. Better agreement is observed in the neighbouring intervals. 
Within this $\met$ interval, Figure~\ref{fig:ch8_vrw_met_study}(\subref{fig:ch8_vrw_met_study_lep_pt}) shows that the excess is concentrated at $300<\pT^\ell<400~\GeV$.
Further checks using different $\met$ thresholds and additional validation regions are described in Appendix~\ref{app:ch8_vrw_excess}.
No indication of a local detector problem is found in the $(\eta,\phi)$ distributions of the leptons and $b$-jets.

Since the probability of observing a $2\sigma$ deviation in one or more of the four VRs is about $17\%$ and the validation results above do not indicate any specific modelling problem, the excess is consistent with a statistical fluctuation.

\subsection[VRTop]{$\VRTop$}
\label{subsec:ch8_vrtop}

After the background-only fit, top-quark production contributes approximately $67\%$ and $86\%$ of the predicted background in $\VRTop$-$\Res$ and $\VRTop$-$\NonRes$, respectively, as shown in Figures~\ref{fig:ch8_vrtop_res} and~\ref{fig:ch8_vrtop_nonres}.
Specifically, single-top and $\ttbar$ production contribute approximately $35\%$ and $32\%$ of the total background in the resonant region, respectively, while $\ttbar$ dominates the non-resonant region with a contribution of approximately $80\%$.
The signal contamination is checked using the same benchmark samples as in $\VRW$. It is $(1.2\pm0.1)\%$ in $\VRTop$-$\Res$ and $(0.6\pm0.03)\%$ in $\VRTop$-$\NonRes$.

After the background-only fit, the expected yields are $13.0\pm3.9$ and $30.1\pm8.1$ in $\VRTop$-$\Res$ and $\VRTop$-$\NonRes$, compared with 19 and 30 observed events, respectively. The observed yields agree with the predictions within about $1\sigma$ in both regions.
The agreement supports the extrapolation from $\CRTop$ to final states without a light lepton.
